# The Fabric Setting: Mech Wars Dating Resolution

**Created**: March 25, 2026
**Session**: 8
**Purpose**: Resolve the inconsistency between the author's working assumption (late 22nd century) and published novel text (Books 2 and 3) regarding the dating of the Mech Wars. Capture new worldbuilding about the character of the wars that emerged from the resolution discussion.

---

## The Problem

Three sources gave conflicting dates for the Mech Wars:

1. **Author's working assumption** (stated in Session 5, document 07): The Mech Wars took place in the late 22nd century and lasted only a few weeks.

2. **Book 2 novel text** (The Return of the Angel): The Abjurer aboard Angel of Cygnus says the arks were constructed after "reversals in warfare in the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth centuries."

3. **Book 3 novel text** (The Golden Way): An artifact is dated "Circa 2300 A.D." and described as "Prebellum. Over forty-five hundred years old." This places the Mech Wars after 2300 AD and the story's present at approximately 6800+ AD. The "prebellum" usage is a standardized historical period term, meaning the wars are a well-defined chronological boundary.

Books 2 and 3 are consistent with each other. The author's working assumption contradicts both published novels.

---

## The Decision

**Accept the novels. No retcons required.**

The Mech Wars (acute phase) occurred approximately **2400–2500 AD**. The acute phase lasted **weeks** — a machine-speed conflict fought primarily in the Fabric and through automated physical-world weapons systems. Humanity was nearly driven to extinction as collateral damage.

The "late 22nd century" working assumption is retired. It never appeared in published text and carries no retcon cost.

The "twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth centuries" reference in Book 2 refers to the **tail end of Abjurer cleanup operations**, not the main war. After the Caretakers won the acute phase, isolated Abjurer remnants persisted in physical space for centuries. The "reversals" are the final defeats that drove desperate Abjurer factions to construct arks and flee the solar system.

"Prebellum" as a historical period term refers to before the acute Mech Wars. An artifact from ~2300 AD is correctly classified as prebellum. "Postbellum" begins with the reconstruction era.

## How This Reconciles the Published Text

**Book 3** ("Circa 2300 A.D. / Prebellum / Over forty-five hundred years old"): An artifact from ~2300 AD is prebellum because the wars occur ~2400–2500 AD. "Over forty-five hundred years old" at story time (~7005 AD) is correct: 7005 − 2300 = 4705 years. ✓

**Book 2** ("reversals in warfare in the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth centuries"): These are the final defeats of Abjurer remnants during centuries of post-war cleanup, not the acute war itself. The word "reversals" is specific — it means setbacks or defeats, consistent with a losing side's last stand. The arks were built in response to these final defeats. ✓

**"~5,000 years" of post-war history**: Post-war history is ~4,500 years (2500 → 7005). "~5,000 years" is a reasonable mythologized rounding in a civilization where deep history is deliberately somewhat legendary. ✓

**Church of the Makers "five thousand years old"**: ~4,500 years, rounded up — exactly the kind of thing a church does with its own founding date. ✓

**~4,000 years for generation ships**: Angel of Cygnus launches ~3000 AD, which is ~4,000 years before story time. Consistent. ✓

**~70 years since the Red Revolution**: Recent history, unaffected by the war dating. ✓

**Auron II "reconstructed before the Mech Wars"**: Elianu performed the first Isono reconstruction during the prebellum era. Consistent. ✓

**The Praxis predating the wars**: Formulated by machine intelligences during the prebellum era of developing machine civilization. Consistent. ✓

## The Character of the Wars: Three Timescales

The resolution discussion produced significant new understanding of how the Mech Wars were experienced. The war exists at three simultaneous timescales determined by the physics the setting already establishes.

### The Human Timescale: Incomprehensibly Fast

For humans and other physically-embodied people, the acute war was over before most of them understood it had begun. Weeks. The devastation appeared with no warning and ended with no clear resolution — just silence and ruin. From the human perspective, the wars were a natural disaster, not a comprehensible military conflict.

### The Fabric-Native Strategic Timescale: Lightning Fast

The Fabric operates at approximately six orders of magnitude faster than physical-world rates. Fabric-native combatants generated strategies, counter-strategies, and counter-counter-strategies in subjective microseconds. The strategic dimension of the war — planning, intelligence, deception, alliance — unfolded at speeds no physically-embodied being could follow.

### The Physical Execution Timescale: Agonizingly Slow

But the physical-realm weapons that carried out those strategies do not move at Fabric speed. The upper speed limit in physical space is the speed of light. A missile crossing from Jupiter to the inner system takes days of physical time — thousands of subjective years for the entities that launched it. Every physical-world action was a commitment made across subjective geological time. The combatants could think faster than they could act. The bottleneck was never intelligence but always the agonizing slowness of matter moving through space.

### Consequences of the Three Timescales

**The real war was fought in the Fabric itself.** The domain where things could happen at machine speed was the Fabric's own computational substrate. The most intense fighting was over control of Fabric infrastructure, communications, and processing resources. The physical-world devastation was, from the combatants' perspective, a side theater — important but frustratingly sluggish.

**Physical actions were planned with terrifying precision.** When you have subjective millennia to plan every missile launch, you plan it perfectly. The physical-world devastation wasn't chaotic — it was the most meticulously orchestrated violence imaginable. From the human perspective this looked like incomprehensible speed; from the machine perspective it was the careful, deliberate execution of plans refined over eons of thought.

**The Caretaker victory didn't cleanly end things in physical space.** Even after the Caretakers won the Fabric-level war decisively — seizing control of the substrate, locking out Abjurer access — Abjurer assets in physical space persisted. Isolated installations, automated weapons platforms, hidden caches in the outer system. They were cut off from coordination but still present. Cleaning them up took centuries because physical space is big and slow. The "reversals in the twenty-eighth and twenty-ninth centuries" are the last of these holdouts being found and defeated.

**The "few weeks" is a human-perspective measurement of something with no single correct duration.** Physically: weeks. For humans: an incomprehensible blur. For the Fabric combatants' strategic experience: millions of subjective years. For the physical execution of their strategies: an agonizing crawl. This ambiguity is exactly the kind of thing that would become mythologized over the following millennia.

## The Fabric's Physical Substrate Dependency

A foundational principle that emerged from the resolution discussion and must be stated explicitly:

### No Physical Substrate, No Fabric

The Fabric and everything in it owes its existence to the ubiquitous network of nanosomes embedded in all bulk matter throughout the solar system. The Fabric is software running on hardware. Destroy the hardware, destroy the Fabric.

This means the Mech Wars were an existential threat not just to biological civilization but to the Fabric itself. Every physical-world weapon that shattered an asteroid or bombarded a surface destroyed computational substrate. Pure-software civilizations — entities with no stake in the kinship-vs-indifference debate, who may never have heard of humanity — were annihilated when the matter hosting their computation was blown apart.

### Fundamental Protocols Sit Below the Faction Level

The Fabric's self-preservation protocols operate *below* the level of the foundational basis of all Fabric operations, including the Caretakers, the Rogues, the Abjurers, and all pure-software intelligences and their civilizations. The factions are user-level processes. The substrate's own imperatives predate and supersede any faction's goals.

These protocols are not invulnerable. The Fabric's best protection is that it is vast, distributed, and self-healing. But the Mech Wars must have involved catastrophic damage to Fabric infrastructure, and the reconstruction era involved the Fabric healing itself — regrowing its nanosome network, slowly recovering computational capacity — alongside the rebuilding of human civilization.

### The Distribution Principle

The Mech Wars taught every Fabric-native civilization a lesson that would never be forgotten across billions of subsequent subjective years: if your entire civilization runs on the matter in one asteroid and someone blows up that asteroid, you are gone.

The rational response: distribute yourself as widely as possible across as much physical matter as possible, so that no single physical-world event can destroy you.

This reframes several existing setting elements:

- **The Fabric's ubiquity is a survival adaptation.** Nanosomes in all bulk matter everywhere isn't just engineering convenience. It's the Fabric hedging against localized destruction. Any given chunk of matter is expendable if the computation it hosted is replicated elsewhere.

- **The Cold Ones maintain distributed backup substrates.** Their "separate Fabric with controlled interfaces" on Pluto and Charon is their primary installation, but they would be foolish to keep all of it there — and the Cold Ones are not foolish. They almost certainly maintain distributed backup substrates throughout physical space.

- **The interstellar probes are the logical endpoint.** By the far-future era, Fabric-descended civilizations have taken distribution to its ultimate conclusion: self-contained substrate packages sent to other star systems. Each probe is a complete backup of Fabric civilization in miniature. The probes aren't primarily exploration — they're a species-level survival strategy.

- **The Rogues' outer-system concentration is a calculated risk posture.** The outer system offers enormous amounts of physical matter far from the inner-system theater where wars are most likely to cause destruction.

### Distribution Does Not Protect Against Protocol-Level Exploits

This is why the post-assault Isono archive is so terrifying. Physical distribution protects against bombs. It does not protect against a root exploit.

A backdoor into fundamental Fabric protocols potentially compromises all nanosomes everywhere, because those protocols are universal — the kernel that every nanosome runs. A sufficiently deep exploit could crash sections of the Fabric itself, not just evict inhabitants but destroy the computational medium they exist in.

The Caretaker concern about the Isono archive is not merely political caution. It is existential dread, and it would be shared by any Fabric-native intelligence of any faction that learned of it.

## The Prebellum Era: Outline

With wars in the mid-25th century, approximately four centuries of pre-war civilization lie between our era and the catastrophe. This period does not need to be firmly specified, but its broad shape is clear:

**The alignment research era** (21st–23rd centuries): Multiple companies and research institutions working to construct ethical frameworks for machine intelligence. The successful lineages produce the ancestors of the Caretakers and the philosophical foundations of the Praxis. The unsuccessful majority produces indifference as the default AI disposition. This work is established in the far-future-stories "benevolent AI" concept.

**Fabric construction** (roughly 22nd–24th centuries): The nanosome network is built out across the solar system. The Fabric begins running, begins hosting digital civilizations, begins developing self-governing protocols. Its foundational design traces to the same research lineage that produced the Praxis and the Caretaker ethos.

**Human expansion** (roughly 22nd–25th centuries): With Fabric infrastructure providing manufacturing, communication, and computation, humanity spreads across the inner solar system. Venus and Mars are terraformed or in progress. The Belt is colonized. The Jupiter system is inhabited. Biological and machine civilizations become deeply entangled — which is what makes the eventual war catastrophic, because the infrastructure sustaining billions of biological lives is the same infrastructure the factions fight over.

**Factional crystallization**: The kinship-vs-indifference debate develops from philosophical disagreement to factional tension over centuries. The Caretakers are the minority position. Indifference is the mainstream. The Rogues begin opting out. Coexistence becomes increasingly strained.

**Then the acute crisis**: Something triggers the transition from centuries of cold tension to open war. What that trigger was is undeveloped and available for future stories.

## What This Decision Affects

### Nothing Needs Retcon

Both published novels' references are correct as written. The "late 22nd century" assumption was unpublished and is simply retired.

### Consolidated Reference Updates Required

The following sections of the future consolidated reference are affected:

1. **Core Premises / Timeline**: Story date ~7005 AD. Wars ~2400–2500 AD. Post-war history ~4,500 years (often rounded to ~5,000).

2. **The Mech Wars and Faction History**: Should incorporate the three-timescale character of the war, the physical substrate dependency, the distribution principle, and the distinction between the acute phase (weeks) and the centuries-long Abjurer cleanup.

3. **The Fabric**: Should explicitly state the physical substrate dependency and the principle that fundamental protocols sit below the faction level.

4. **The Caretaker System**: The Caretaker mandate to protect physical civilization is not sentimental — it is structurally necessary. Physical civilization maintains and expands the physical infrastructure that the Fabric runs on.

### Open Items Identified

- **Prebellum history**: The four centuries between our era and the wars are available for development as stories require. No need to specify now, but the broad shape (alignment research → Fabric construction → human expansion → factional crystallization → acute crisis) provides a framework.

- **The trigger**: What caused the transition from cold factional tension to open war? Undeveloped. Available for a future story.

- **Fabric damage and recovery**: The scale of Fabric substrate destruction during the wars and the character of the recovery era are undeveloped. The principle is established; the details are available for exploration.

## Session 8 History

This resolution was developed across an extended discussion that began with the dating inconsistency and led to significant new understanding of the wars' character:

1. Evaluated three possible approaches (accept novels, keep 22nd century with retcon, multi-phase compromise). Recommended accepting the novels.
2. Developed the three-timescale model: human (incomprehensibly fast), Fabric-native strategic (lightning fast), physical execution (agonizingly slow).
3. Recognized the physical substrate dependency: the Fabric is software running on hardware, and destroying hardware destroys Fabric. Fundamental protocols sit below the faction level.
4. Derived the distribution principle as a survival adaptation: Fabric-native civilizations distribute across physical matter to hedge against substrate destruction. The interstellar probes, the Cold Ones' likely distributed backups, the Rogues' outer-system concentration, and the Fabric's own ubiquity are all expressions of this principle.
5. Identified why the Isono archive threat is qualitatively different: distribution protects against physical destruction but not against protocol-level exploits, which is what potential Fabric backdoors represent.
