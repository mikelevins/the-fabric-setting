# Projectile Weapons and Fabric-Assisted Aiming

**Source**: mikel, 2026-09-04 (Book 4 sitting; ruled while deciding whether a Doctrine man fires during the Port Robinson chase). Ruled canon; derived items marked.
**Scope**: system-wide.

---

## Aiming

**Almost all projectile weapons in the Fabric use Fabric-assisted aiming, which essentially never misses.** Not strictly never: a sudden strong gust can move a projectile more than calculated, or a sudden movement can take a target out of the line of fire faster than magnetic trajectory-trimming can correct. But misses are much, much rarer than in the present-day real world. **For narrative purposes, if someone shoots, assume they hit exactly what they aimed at.**

*Consequences for writing (derived):*
- There is no "warning shot that wrecks furniture and hits no one." A fired weapon is a hit, and a hit has to be dealt with on the page.
- A shot is therefore never free percussion. Every discharge is a decision with consequences, and it should be motivated like one.
- A threat to shoot that is not executed is itself informative: it tells the reader what the shooter knows they cannot afford.

## Rounds

**The default round in a policing or coercive context is a compliance round, not a lethal one.** A compliance round stops a biological target's flight cold; it is not lethal to a person and does nothing lethal to a Mech. *(Open: what a compliance round does to a Mech at all — nothing, or a stop — is unruled.)*

## Open questions (ask, don't confabulate)

- What, if anything, a compliance round does to a Mech.
