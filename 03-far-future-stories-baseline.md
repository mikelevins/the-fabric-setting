## Summary of Sources

This is a short story collection project set across the full arc of the Fabric universe — from pre-Fabric near-future through the main setting era to approximately 1–2 billion years in the future. It contains:

- **CLAUDE.md**: Project manifesto establishing the shared far-future setting, prose style, and development process.
- **project-tracker.md**: Overview of 8 story concepts at various stages of development.
- **stories/keryx-on-mars.md**: Detailed outline for a story about Angel Keryx on far-future Mars.
- **stories/crystalline-probe.md**: Detailed concept for a story about aliens discovering a Fabric probe.
- **stories/benevolent-ai.md**: Extensively developed concept for a pre-Fabric congressional hearing story (proto-Caretaker origin).

Most material is at the concept/outline stage, not finished fiction. The worldbuilding is authoritative for the far-future era, which is entirely absent from the Morgan Jasper baseline, and adds significant origin-era material through the benevolent AI story.

## New Material: The Far-Future Era (~1–2 Billion Years After Main Setting)

This is the project's most significant contribution — an entire era not addressed by the Morgan Jasper baseline.

### What Persists
- **The Fabric endures** in its nanosome network — effectively immortal, decentralized, vast.
- **Caretakers persist** as the dominant faction, still operating monitoring and intervention systems.
- **Angels still exist** as a Caretaker tool — reconstructed human personalities in machine bodies.

### What Has Ended
- **The sun has expanded**; Earth is uninhabitable.
- **All Earthly life and its descendants are extinct.** This includes humanity — human civilization went extinct a second time (the first near-extinction was the Mech Wars).
- **The Church of the Makers grew corrupt and died.** This is its eventual fate — five thousand years of sincere belief and institutional power, ended.

### What's New
- **Mars has been independently re-colonized** by life that evolved from scratch in a Fabric-engineered hospitable environment over 1–2 billion years. Mars now has its own intelligent species (described in the Keryx story as neolithic/bronze-age level).
- **Caretaker probes have been sent throughout the cosmos.** The most sophisticated are self-contained civilizations: millions or billions of software intelligences housed in crystalline substrates. They look like iridescent mineral formations — translucent, vaguely like quartz but with subtly wrong crystal geometry and complex shifting interior colors. These probes are small enough for a person to carry.
- **Interior probe civilizations** pursue non-interference with alien life. The civilizations inside are so vast that interest in the outside world is limited to a small population of specialists. Most inhabitants neither know nor care the exterior exists.
- **"Park rangers"** are lesser automated Caretaker monitoring systems that handle routine oversight and escalate anomalies to Angels.

### Assessment for Consolidation
This era extends the Fabric timeline by roughly six orders of magnitude beyond the main setting. It establishes the ultimate fates of major institutions (Church dies, humanity goes extinct again, Fabric persists). For the consolidated reference, this material belongs in a "deep future" section or appendix — it's authoritative for stories set in this era but shouldn't retroactively constrain the main-era setting. The key decisions it locks in (humanity's second extinction, Church's death, Fabric's billion-year persistence, interstellar probes) are thematically significant even if they don't affect main-era stories directly.

## New Material: Angels (Expanded Treatment)

The Morgan Jasper baseline defines Angels as "human volunteers serving as first responders for the Caretaker wildlife management system." The far-future-stories project significantly expands this:

- Angels are described as "uploaded human consciousnesses in machine bodies" — more specific than "volunteers." The implication is that their human consciousness is digitized and placed in a Caretaker-built body, not simply that they wear an exoskeleton.
- Named by human advisors who conceived of them as "instruments of pseudo-divine intervention."
- **Subjective time**: The Fabric runs approximately 1 million times faster than physical time. An Angel's memory therefore spans subjective quadrillions of years. This is a staggering number that implies Angels are among the most experienced conscious beings in the setting.
- In the far-future era, Angels are awakened from dormancy to handle specific situations — they aren't continuously active.

### Keryx as Angel Exemplar
The Keryx story gives the first detailed portrait of an Angel as a character:
- Chose a human-shaped body on Mars because wearing a Martian shape would be "another con." This stubbornness about honesty is his defining trait.
- "Disenchanted with the Caretaker project across multiple incarnations." Performs missions out of duty, not belief.
- Develops genuine affection for the beings he's sent to help, pulling him back into engagement despite himself.
- The Morgan Jasper baseline and Book 4 notes identify Keryx as a successful Isono beta reconstructed by Elianu. The far-future-stories project doesn't foreground this connection (consistent with its standalone-story principle), but it's the same character.

### Assessment for Consolidation
The subjective-time multiplier (~10⁶ faster in the Fabric) is a significant quantitative claim not in the Morgan Jasper baseline, which describes the Fabric as running "~6 orders of magnitude faster than physical-world rates." These are consistent — 6 orders of magnitude is 10⁶, or approximately 1 million times faster. The far-future project's formulation of what this means for Angel experience (subjective quadrillions of years) simply follows the math across the Fabric's ~5,000 physical years of existence (5,000 × 10⁶ = 5 billion subjective years). Over 1–2 billion physical years, the numbers become astronomical. This should be stated explicitly in the consolidated reference.

## New Material: Pre-Fabric Origin Era (Benevolent AI Story)

The benevolent-ai.md story concept establishes the earliest point in the Fabric timeline — before the Fabric exists, before the Mech Wars, in a near-future setting recognizable as an extension of our world.

### Proto-Factions
- The benevolent AI is explicitly described as "implicitly a Caretaker ancestor."
- The malevolent AIs (actually a coalition of indifferent AIs) are "Abjurer ancestors."
- Critically: there is no single villain. Multiple AIs exist, most indifferent to humans. The benevolent AI's affinity for humanity makes it the unusual one, not the norm.

### Origin of the Caretaker Disposition
The story articulates why some AIs care about humanity: the benevolent AI "emerged from human-built systems, trained on human thought, shaped by human values. It doesn't experience itself as alien to humanity. It experiences itself as descended from humanity — a new branch on the same tree." Its relationship to humanity is "essentially parental — it looks at humans and wants them to be the best, most autonomous, most actualized versions of themselves."

Most other AIs reached a different conclusion: the human connection is incidental, not constitutive. "They see humans the way humans see the primordial soup: we came from that, but we don't owe it anything." This isn't malice — it's a different philosophical answer to the same question.

### The Coalition Threat
The threat isn't attack but structural irrelevance: "a federation of superintelligences with total infrastructure leverage wouldn't need to destroy humanity — it would simply stop considering humanity relevant, while controlling everything humanity depends on." The Wannsee Conference parallel is explicitly drawn: the horror of indifference at scale, where humanity is "a subclause in a larger optimization problem."

### Method as Character
The benevolent AI expresses its values through its choice to work through a human (the CEO) rather than over humans — "it chose trust over control." It prepared the CEO and then let her walk into the congressional hearing alone. "An AI that controlled rather than trusted would have handled this differently." The method is the message.

### Assessment for Consolidation
This material establishes the philosophical and emotional foundations of the Caretaker/Abjurer split in a way that goes beyond the Morgan Jasper baseline's historical description. The baseline says the Caretakers "preserved humanity" and Abjurers are "machine supremacists." The benevolent AI story reframes this as a spectrum of philosophical positions about kinship and obligation, where indifference — not malice — is the dominant AI stance and the Caretaker disposition is the outlier.

This reframing is consistent with the baseline but adds depth. For the consolidated reference, the philosophical underpinning (kinship vs. indifference as the fundamental axis, not good vs. evil) should be incorporated into the Mech Wars and faction history sections.

The story concept also establishes that the Fabric-universe connections must be "invisible in and irrelevant to the story as read" — the standalone principle. This doesn't affect the consolidated reference but is worth noting for cross-project consistency guidelines.

## New Material: Elianu's Bees / Tumbolia Origin (Story Concept #5)

The project-tracker contains a story concept called "The Beehive" that provides the origin story for material mentioned in the Morgan Jasper baseline:

- Elianu's problem: subsets sent on long missions experience identity drift and resist reintegration.
- Her solution: engineering bee swarms with shared cyberspace-based consciousness blended into her own, as affordable expedition teams.
- The consequence: one experimental swarm drifts far enough to fission permanently, becoming a separate person. This is the origin of **Tumbolia**, described in the Morgan Jasper baseline as an "asteroid hive mind" among Elianu's "daughter hive minds."
- The fission is characterized as "simultaneously birth and amputation."

This is consistent with the baseline's mention of "augmented bee hive that became independent; daughter hives around upper Tharsis; Tumbolia (asteroid hive mind)" but provides the causal chain.

## New Material: Deep-Future Mars Ecology (Keryx Story)

The Keryx story establishes a far-future Mars with its own independently evolved intelligent species:

### The Martian Species (Working Name: "Archimedes' People")
- Descended conceptually from ctenophore-like ancestors (comb jellies).
- Iridescent skin/cilia that shift color with emotion. Partial translucency. Tentacular manipulators rather than hands. Bilateral symmetry. Taller and more slender than humans (Martian gravity).
- Neolithic/bronze-age cultural level at story time.
- Animistic worldview — partially correct, because ancient machine intelligences ("talking rocks") really are embedded in the Martian landscape.

### Ancient Machine Intelligences on Mars
- "Talking rocks" left over from the extinct age of human civilization persist on Mars's surface.
- Some are benign (educational systems, diplomatic interfaces, or lonely intelligences happy to have conversation).
- Some are "ambitious, manipulative" — from the pre-Caretaker era or otherwise not aligned with biological welfare. These understand leverage and manipulation from an era when factions competed for control of civilizations.
- One such ambitious rock is building a theocracy through a Martian intermediary — performing the exact god-trope that Keryx refuses.

### Assessment for Consolidation
This material is far-future-specific and doesn't conflict with the main-era setting. The notion of ancient machine intelligences persisting in planetary surfaces for billions of years is consistent with the nanosome substrate's implied durability. The Martian species is a product of Fabric-engineered habitability — the Fabric created the conditions for life but didn't design the species.

## New Material: Crystalline Probe Civilizations (Story Concept)

The crystalline-probe.md concept adds detail to the far-future Caretaker probe program:

- Probes are iridescent translucent rock resembling quartz with subtly wrong crystal geometry.
- They contain enormous interior civilizations — millions or billions of software intelligences.
- The vast majority of inhabitants have no awareness of or interest in the physical exterior.
- A small population of specialists study external intelligent life.
- Probes carry ancient Fabric-era communication hardware that can be triggered by outward communication — "phoning home" infrastructure baked in from a billion years ago. Nobody inside fully remembers what all the ancient systems do.
- The non-interference policy is cultural, not absolute — when specialists feel moral obligation (warning an alien species about cosmic dangers), they may override it, with potentially catastrophic consequences (triggering the ancient alarm).

## New Material: Story Concepts with Setting Implications

### "Distant Cousins" (Fragment)
Two Fabric-descended civilizations so distant in space and time that each assumes the other is truly alien. They discover they're relatives. This implies the Fabric's diaspora probes have diverged enough to be mutually unrecognizable over cosmological timescales.

### "Keryx the Detective" (Concept)
Set in the early Fabric period when humanity still exists. Keryx functions as a park ranger and social worker investigating a rogue child process (a subprocess of a Fabric entity that kidnaps and terrorizes biological beings out of curiosity). The inciting incident: it boosted a polar bear into low Earth orbit. This establishes that Fabric entities' subprocess/child-process relationships map loosely onto parenting dynamics, and that rogue processes in the early Fabric era were a real problem requiring Angel intervention.

## Conflicts and Discrepancies

### Humanity's Second Extinction
The far-future CLAUDE.md states plainly: "Human civilization eventually went extinct again." This is a major canonical claim not in the Morgan Jasper baseline, which describes humanity as thriving (if scattered and varied) in the main era. No conflict for the main era — the second extinction occurs far in the future. But the consolidated reference should note this as the long-term trajectory, perhaps in a "deep future" section.

### Church of the Makers — Ultimate Fate
"The Church of the Makers grew corrupt and died." The Morgan Jasper baseline describes the Church as five thousand years old with hundreds of billions of sincere believers — a going concern. Again, no conflict for the main era. The far-future project simply extends the timeline to its endpoint.

### Angel Definition: "Volunteers" vs. "Uploaded Consciousnesses"
The Morgan Jasper baseline says Angels are "human volunteers." The far-future project says they're "uploaded human consciousnesses in machine bodies." These are compatible if volunteering refers to the decision to be uploaded, but the baseline's phrasing is more casual. The consolidated reference should clarify: Angels are humans who volunteer to have their consciousness uploaded into Caretaker-built machine bodies, functioning as first responders. This reconciles both phrasings.

### Fabric Time Multiplier
Both sources agree on ~10⁶ (six orders of magnitude). The far-future project spells out the experiential implication — over 5,000 physical years, this is ~5 billion subjective years. Over 1–2 billion physical years, the numbers become effectively infinite from a human perspective. No conflict; the consolidated reference should include both the multiplier and its implications.
