# Reproduction, Children, and Schooling in Fabric Societies

**Source**: mikel, 2026-09-02 (Book 4 platform-population sitting). Ruled canon; derived items marked.
**Scope**: the whole collection of Fabric-based societies in the solar system, with Mars and its space elevators as the worked example. Extends the "Rarity of Children" note in `01-morgan-jasper-baseline.md` and "Children and Virtual Childhood" in `05-book4-novel-scan.md`.

---

## Reproduction

- **Children are rare in Fabric society.** Most people reproduce by construction; offspring are most commonly matured in the Fabric (virtual childhood) and embodied fully grown.
- **Origin:** humans nearly went extinct. The population-recovery program introduced reproduction by construction, so **children were absent for at least decades** at the program's start and were reintroduced later, at different times in different recovering populations. Different populations therefore evolved **different approaches to schooling** — there is no system-wide model.
- **Natural child rearing** — giving birth (or constructing in an infant state) and raising the offspring to maturity already embodied — is a minority practice system-wide but the norm in some societies.

## Mars, the largest exception

- **More than half of Martians prefer natural child rearing.** Mars is the largest body and largest population where it is the norm.
- **The space elevators and their spacedocks slant heavily toward the Fabric convention** of reproduction by construction: more children per capita than most societies in the system, far fewer than on the Martian surface.

## Why schools as the 21st century knew them are essentially nonexistent

The Prussian model — age cohorts, orderly classrooms — served purposes the Fabric has removed or transformed:

- **Reading, writing, arithmetic:** the general population has no need of them; the Fabric performs those functions as needed, on demand. Anyone who needs or wants a skill has the knowledge injected directly into the brain; all that remains is **routine practice to seat the neuromuscular coordination**.
- **Obedience to authority figures and social norms in groups:** likewise imparted directly. What remains is **practicing actual behavior — body movement — in groups.**
- **Custody** (parking children somewhere reasonably safe while parents are occupied, e.g., at work): in most Fabric societies **delegated to arbeiters designed for the purpose.**
- **Prosocial convening** survives: a society may still gather children into classrooms to teach the **norms and mores of a social group** — the Participants, a trade or community association, the Church.

**Net:** schools in the traditional sense are essentially nonexistent on Mars and its elevators (and, by the same logic, across most Fabric societies), though something resembling **daycare** does exist.

## Custody on Mars specifically

Martians are generally hostile to nonhumanoid sophonts but **tolerate arbeiters**. Children whose parents can't watch them are therefore either

- **at home with a dedicated arbeiter**, or
- for parents less prosperous or more community-focused, in **daycare facilities provided by sureties, contract facilities, or social institutions such as the Church** — operated by arbeiters, probably under the overall supervision of a human overseer.

## Derived (Claude, 9/2 — not canon)

- The surviving "classroom" is norm-formation, not literacy. **Church-run daycare is therefore a formation pipeline**, consistent with the scan canon that being raised in the Church produces lasting attachment even in people who later leave it.
- Children raised on a 0.06 g elevator platform practice group movement in the wrong gravity for the surface; elevator-born kids would be **gravity-exotic** on Mars.
- Population arithmetic: removing schools takes the human labor for childcare in a town of 1,000–3,000 children down to **a few dozen overseers** rather than a school system. (Book 4's Port Robinson estimate: `Book4/reference/mars.md` pointer → this file.)
