# Cooperation and the "Me First" Factions

**Source**: mikel, 2026-10-02 (Book 5 seed sitting; ruled while working out why a cornered Abjurer cannot trust its own instruments), building on the Book 4 constitution's second theme (mikel, 2026-09-27). Ruled canon; derived items marked.
**Scope**: setting-wide — a theme that keeps coming up across the whole setting, not a device of any one story.

---

## The thesis

**Cooperation beats pure competition, because cooperation, with all its flaws and weaknesses, mobilizes resources that pure competition cannot match.** It is a statement of the supremacy of **generous tit-for-tat in the iterated prisoner's dilemma**.

**The strategy (mikel, 9/27; Book 4 constitution):** start by trusting the other party; punish only if they defect; and, being generous, leave the way back open. The losing alternative is the move that wins only when the game is *not* iterated — the self-defeating posture that always assumes bad faith.

## The "me first" factions and their shared hazard — CANON (mikel, 10/2)

Three factions in the setting embrace a philosophy that **regards trustworthiness as a tactical weakness**:

- **The Abjurers**, who exist because of a formative "anything goes" moral posture;
- **the lineage** (Book 4);
- **the Titans** (see `05-book4-novel-scan.md`, Cooperation Dynamics: Titans know cooperation would be optimal and often propose it, but none of it survives the first clear chance to defect, and much of its surplus is spent on monitoring).

**The hazard is parallel in all three:** because trustworthiness is a weakness in their philosophy, **relying on allies and loyal minions is always a risky proposition.** They have no such thing as a trusted minion.

**The hedge and its cost.** A "me first" principal can guard against betrayal by **denying its agents full access to its resources.** That works as a safety measure, but at a high cost: **its agents are intentionally crippled.** A cooperative principal can do what it cannot — grant its loyal agents unrestricted access. *(Worked case: the Cold Ones, who treat their intermediaries very well and can trust them with their resources, against a cornered Abjurer whose hired instruments it must keep on a short leash — `kestrel/book5-seeds.md`.)*

**Instruments of its own are no escape.** A "me first" entity can build its own instrument, but that instrument is a minion that can theoretically be turned, or develop a competing agenda of its own (mikel, 10/2).

## Where it shows up

At several scales (Book 4 constitution, 9/27):

- **Angier against Leukas** — Angier learned the strategy from Rayleigh out of pure enlightened self-interest.
- **Elianu against the lineage** — she gives every instance the chance to show it has learned the right solution.
- **Jove against Mars** — mutual aid builds a robust society; paranoia and an extractive mentality build a patchwork tearing itself apart.
- **The Caretakers against the Abjurers** — care and generosity build an ecosystem that grows in population and diversity; predatory paranoia builds a dystopian nightmare that feeds on itself.

*Derived (unruled):* the flaw is a matter of **philosophy, not wiring.** Angier — a sociopath by constitution — is reliable because he is invested in his team's prosperity; the "me first" factions fail because of what they believe about trust, not because of what kind of minds they are.

*Banked for Book 5, not setting canon:* the **Rogues** as the theme's middle term — neither cooperative on principle like the Caretakers nor defecting on principle like the Abjurers; they simply trade and reciprocate because it works, which makes them the strongest evidence that cooperation wins even among those who don't believe in it (`kestrel/book5-seeds.md` §The Rogues).
