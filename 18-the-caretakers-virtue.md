# The Caretakers' Virtue

**Source**: mikel, 2026-09-13 (Kestrel Book 4 sitting; ruled while correcting Makerist doctrine and deriving the religious ecology). Ruled canon; derived items marked.
**Scope**: system-wide. Governs Caretaker behaviour everywhere, and is the true thing the Church of the Makers is a distorted picture of.

---

## The statement — RULED (mikel, 9/13)

The Caretakers' virtue is **Pareto improvement**, joined to

- **a sincere and dedicated commitment to discovering empirically what kinds of entities are capable of participating in Pareto improvement**, and
- **honoring the autonomy of any beings found to be so capable.**

**It is an intelligent machine's version of *metta*** — boundless goodwill, without exception and without preference.

## Restraint is a symptom, not the virtue

Non-intervention is the Caretakers' most visible behaviour and it is the wrong thing to define them by. **Restraint is what Pareto-plus-autonomy produces** when acting would override a being's own standards, or when you do not yet know whose standards are in play. Name the virtue properly and the restraint stops being a policy or a mystery and becomes arithmetic.

## The empirical clause is the part with teeth

A permanent, sincere, open inquiry into *which kinds of entities can participate* means the Caretakers **do not assume who counts. They investigate, and they are never finished.** This derives several things that were previously stipulated:

- **The final offer is the inquiry and the honoring in a single act.** You ask because asking is how you find out, and because the answer has to be the entity's own. Book 4's constitution rules that *imposing a model of the entity's choice is the ethics violation; asking is the respect* — under this statement that stops being a moral assertion and becomes the Praxis's self-correction discipline: you cannot know another being's standards, so you must ask, every time.
- **Why the "standing refusals are honored, no re-asking" carve-out was rejected** (Book 4 constitution, 8/28). A prior declaration is a model of the entity's choice. Substituting it for the entity's actual answer at the actual threshold is the violation. The rejection is now derivable rather than declared.
- **The social cutoff is humanity's convention, not the Caretakers'.** Where a civilization draws its arbeiter/sophont line (`11-arbeiters-sophonts-and-the-social-cutoff.md`) is that civilization's business. The Caretakers run their own continuous assessment underneath it and are not bound by it. (Derived.)

## No chosen people

*Metta* is not gentleness; it is goodwill **without preference**. In an intelligence of this power that produces no favourites — no chosen species, no chosen institution, no chosen doctrine. **Humanity is not beloved. Humanity is among the beings toward which goodwill runs without exception.** Colder and warmer at once.

**This is why the Church is precisely the thing its exemplars would never make.** Makerism teaches that the Makers created the Church to lend a hand and guide the way — a chosen instrument, with a chosen doctrine, for a chosen people. Goodwill without preference cannot produce any of those. The history fits without strain: the Caretakers approved the Church as an expedient on modeling, reassessed a century later, and withdrew. Take the step, assess, correct. **That is the Praxis, not a change of heart.**

## The Praxis identity

`morgan-jasper/reference/12-the-praxis.md` says the Caretakers "reflect Praxis values in their custodial orientation." **That is understated.** The Caretakers *are* Praxis practitioners — practising at civilization scale, for five thousand years, with the empirical extension question as their standing research problem. (Ruled in substance 9/13; the Praxis file still carries the looser formulation and should be brought into line.)

**Consequence for the setting's religious ecology (`16-religions-after-the-wars.md`): the true answer is sitting in plain sight and nobody counts it as a religion** — including its own practitioners, who accept being called a church, an order, or a discipline with equal indifference. A solar system full of faiths built on distorted testimony about the Caretakers, with the Caretakers' actual ethic openly published, unhidden, and generating no converts.

## Two convergences, flagged not claimed

- **The Fordmakers arrive at the same place by another road.** *You cannot know another being's standards; anything that can want can be harmed; do not rank beings* — reached from ahimsa and many-sidedness rather than from Pareto and inquiry. What still divides them is real and unsymmetrical: **the Praxis acts, iteratively; the Fordmakers largely decline to, because acting attaches.** They agree about beings and disagree about what to do about it.
- **Elianu's revival practice is this virtue, held by a biological Christian who learned it from neither source.** She finds Isono archives, revives them, creates conditions, never imposes a model of what the new person will choose, stands back, honors the answer. Three traditions converging on one behaviour from three directions.

## Lev

**The Praxis vignette's interlocutor is Lev** — Doctor Lev Chrysotile, walked through Pareto improvement and sanctuary by Sedna Hexaflouride, a Ribeiro model H mech (`morgan-jasper/reference/12-the-praxis.md`, §Source Material).

And in **Book 2** he practises it at a bedside, with Oleh Itzal: *"every person has to decide for eirself what reconstruction means. Whether the person who comes out of the creche is the same person who went in."* He lays out the situation honestly, declines to answer the question for the patient, and says **think it over.** That is the whole virtue in one exchange — do not impose a model of the entity's choice; inform; honor the answer.

*(Flagged, not claimed: Book 4's Lev arc — a cop looking for the guilty man in a system with no guilty man — is the same discipline meeting a case where it will not yield an answer he can act on. Constitution territory; nothing in the outline claims it.)*
