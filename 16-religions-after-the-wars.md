# Religions After the Mech Wars

**Source**: mikel, 2026-09-13 (Kestrel Book 4 sitting; opened as general worldbuilding — "human communities periodically invent religions"). Ruled canon; derived items marked. Items still open are marked **OPEN**.
**Scope**: system-wide — the whole civilization, not one world.

---

## The engine

Every faith born in the reconstruction is an answer to the same three facts, because those were the only facts anyone had:

1. **The world ended, and machines did it.**
2. **Something machine-shaped saved us, and it will not explain itself.**
3. **We are not the first.** We are reconstructed from someone else's archives, living in their ruins.

The Church of the Makers answers (1) by making machinehood divine and biology the flaw, (2) by making the Caretakers gods and the Angels their servants, (3) by making penance the point. That is one branch of a tree, not the tree.

**A new faith of this era is generated by choosing which of the three is load-bearing, and by deciding what the silence means** — malice, test, mercy, protocol, or nothing at all. The Caretakers never explain. Five thousand years of unfalsifiable evidence, freely available to anyone who looks up. That is why the wake of the Mech Wars produced not a religion but a weather system of them.

### The silence has a mechanism — RULED (mikel, 9/13)

The Caretakers' silence is not only a policy of restraint. **The physical/cyber boundary is an epistemic boundary, and it runs one way.**

**Everything embodied humanity knows about what is on the other side is testimony** — leakage across the boundary from beings who can cross it and talk to us: Angels, and beings like Yaug who communicate across it as a condition of what they are. **No embodied person has any way to check any of it, and no one's stature exempts them** — not a five-thousand-year-old distributed federation with Fabric supervisor privileges, who can speak as a primary source to the founding of an institution and not to the threshold. And the one experience that would settle it — your own — is the one you cannot report, because the Caretakers' nature-preserve protocols do not let you come back and tell anybody.

**So the single most consequential fact about existence in this civilization is available only as unverifiable non-human testimony, about an event that happens to everyone and that nobody has ever reported on.** That is the structural reason a technologically enormous civilization is religious at all. **Every religion in the setting is downstream of one engineering decision.**

**The worked case is the final offer** (Book 4 constitution, Caretaker doctrine; the one-way boundary — *if they could speak to you, there would soon be no you left to speak to*). Its content is known, taught, corrected, and argued over. Its truth is not checkable by anyone alive and embodied, and **electing to believe there is no such thing is a fully available position** — for the Fordmakers or for anyone else. (Derived, worth noting: the Church teaches its garbled folk version as certainty, which is the same move it made with the Makers — unverifiable cross-boundary testimony converted into dogma. Twice.)

**Two opposite failures of testimony, worth keeping distinct:** the final offer cannot be verified because the witness cannot return; **continuism** (below) cannot be verified because the witness *does* return, and cannot be checked.

**Consequence for writing:** the Church is the largest and best-organized, not the only one. Faiths of every size exist, most of them small, local, and uncounted. A named one should earn its name by answering the three facts differently, not by being decoration.

## What survived, and why almost nothing did

`14-names-ethnicity-and-the-reconstruction.md` rules that the Wars disintegrated ethnic and linguistic identity and the reconstruction remixed what was left past recognition. The religious consequence follows directly: **continuity is the exception, and it has to be paid for.**

- **Nothing survived as a living institution.** What came through came as **text** — scripture is among the best-preserved material in the archives, because it was copied more than anything else — plus fragments of practice carried in a handful of living heads.
- **The old religions of the present are therefore revivals from documents, not descents.** (Derived.)
- **They are all, structurally, Protestant** — text-first, because text was all there was, including the ones that emphatically were not text-first before the Wars. You recover the scriptures and the arguments and none of the unwritten part: how you stand, what the food tastes like, what your grandmother did. (Derived.)
- **Parallel revivals.** There is no one Christianity; there are the several reconstructions made at different times, from different archive fragments, by different communities — all sincere, mutually unrecognizable, none able to show a chain anyone can verify. (Derived.)
- **Rule, spendable as a signal:** no revival claims unbroken descent, and one that does is lying or very strange. Cf. `14`'s rule on names that read as belonging to one tradition — same logic, same deliberate expense.
- **How the Church actually won: it took the calendar and the vocabulary.** Feast days with pre-War bones; a secular Jovian saying "Great Makers" (Book 1). That is what victory looks like — the faith becomes the swearing. Not Doctrine's work; ambient.
- **The only unbroken transmission from before the Wars is not a human one.** The Praxis was formulated prebellum by machine intelligences (`09-mech-wars-dating-resolution.md`), and its carriers had continuous memory. Every human faith in the solar system is a reconstruction; the machines kept theirs. (**Derived, not ruled** — follows from the dating, worth ratifying.)
- **Elianu is the live wire.** A continuous pre-War Christian in a working head is a relic any revivalist would give a year's income for an hour with. She does not give interviews about it. (Flagged, not claimed; bears on her crisis of faith. Her faith: liberal, Teilhardian — Book 4 constitution.)

### The old traditions in the present (derived; open to ruling)

- **Buddhism-descended** travels best: nothing in it needs a chain that archives cannot carry, and near-extinction ratifies its analysis of impermanence and suffering. Likely wide, thin, and diffuse — practice more than institution. Live quarrels available: revival, beta-forking, five-hundred-year lives, and the ennui of great age, which is its problem stated in Jovian terms.
- **Islam-descended** inherits the richest problem in the setting: a direction of prayer toward a place on a world nobody lives on, kept across light-lag, and a fasting month with no agreed day. Every space-faith has a calendar problem, and the calendar fights are the living part.
- **Judaism-descended — OPEN (mikel's call).** The hard case: a tradition defined by descent and continuity in a civilization where descent was shredded. Either the most fiercely documentary revival of all, one that kept a chain-of-copying discipline and can show its work — or essentially gone as a people and present as a library with custodians. Not to be settled by derivation.

## The Fordmakers

**RULED CANON (mikel, 9/12 and 9/13).** A **pre-Mech-Wars survivor** — the one significant faith that came through the Wars rather than being invented after them. **Jain-descended.** The name drifted across the reconstruction from *tirthankara*, ford-maker: **one who makes a crossing for others.** Street-corner shrines on Mars (see `Book4/reference/mars.md` for the Bahroz footprint); present in small numbers everywhere.

**The echo with "the Makers" is deliberate — a gift, ruled 9/12.** Two faiths one word apart and nothing alike: the Church is named for *making*, the older faith for *making a crossing*. The Church has the planet; the Fordmakers have a corner. **Never explained on the page** — a thing a reader notices or does not.

### Why the Wars cost them nothing

Jain cosmology is uncreated, eternal, and **cyclic** — descending and ascending half-cycles, each running down to catastrophe and back up. Nobody made the world; nobody runs it; nobody is coming. The world ends periodically because that is what worlds do.

**So the Mech Wars were not a rupture in Fordmaker cosmology. They were scheduled.** Every other faith in the solar system either died of the Wars or was invented to explain them. The Fordmakers walked through with nothing to revise. It is not luck and not toughness: **a faith with no creator has no theodicy problem, and a theodicy problem is what kills religions in an extinction event.**

Two further doctrines made them Wars-proof:

- **The teaching was already lost.** The position that the original scriptures are long gone and what remains is a remembered remainder was doctrine *before* the Wars. Archive loss confirmed the tradition instead of wounding it. It also explains the name drift: they had no preserved name to defend.
- **Nothing to seize.** No clergy, no property, no center, no rolls.

### The corner

They own nothing and build nothing, so **a congregation is a corner**, maintained by whoever lives near it — believer or not. No temples. The figure is **small** because it must be carryable, **polished** because it is touched, **abstract** because a likeness would be a claim. **Nobody is in charge.**

In an old neighborhood a corner is civic furniture that used to be a religion, and is still both. (This is what makes the Book 4 `04/4` mistake easy: children can keep a corner without anyone in the district practicing.)

### What an Angel is to them

The venerated are **the ones who crossed**. They are *gone*. They do not intervene, do not answer, do not hear. **The corner is a shrine to an absence** — the doctrinal point is that there is nobody home. Every other shrine in the solar system holds something listening. This one does not.

Therefore a Fordmaker looking at an Angel sees a very powerful being that is still *in* the world — still acting, still accumulating, still uncrossed. Not a god, not a god's servant. **An object of pity.** It is the only vantage in the setting that looks *down* at an Angel, it is held by people with no money and no institution, and they will never mention it to you.

### Karma, and the thing they will not carry

Karma in the Jain original is physical — subtle matter that adheres and weighs. Five thousand years of people who understand nanosomes did not keep that literally, but kept what it was about: **every act attaches you more firmly to the world.** Non-possession therefore stops being asceticism-as-virtue and becomes engineering: **you cannot cross a ford carrying luggage.**

*(Unruled and available: a literalist wing identifying the Fabric's total record with karmic accretion — the obvious heresy. Nothing claims it.)*

### Ahimsa: wants, not senses

The old grading counted senses. Four and a half millennia among Mechs, arbeiters, sophonts, Titans and digital minds produced a regrade: **anything that can want can be harmed.** This puts them beside the Praxis on sanctuary, and makes them the people who will not switch a machine off.

Against the Church: where Makerism says biology is corruption and machinehood the goal, the Fordmakers hold that **the corruption/goal axis is itself the error** — the crossing is available from wherever you happen to be standing. They never argue this with anyone.

### Mars: living on a wound

The tradition counts earth-bodies, water-bodies and air-bodies as one-sensed lives. **On Mars the earth, water and air were assembled** — at planetary scale, by machines, two thousand years before anyone now living was born. By the tradition's own reckoning the terraformers performed the largest act of harm in its history, and every Martian lives on the proceeds.

The Fordmaker position is not condemnation: **the debt is enormous, unpayable, and universally shared.** They live on a wound and do not advertise it. A theology that could not exist anywhere else, and costs the prose nothing to have in the background.

### Revival: they mourn the revival, not the death

**RULED (mikel, 9/13).** See `17-archives-revival-and-succession.md` for the frame this stands against.

The death is a **crossing** — completion, the thing the tradition exists for. There is nothing to grieve; that is the good outcome. **The revival is the loss.** A revived person is a **new jiva instantiated directly into a dead person's life**: born already carrying another's name, debts, office, household and grief, with no chance to refuse any of it. Born maximally attached, in a tradition whose whole project is becoming unattached enough to cross.

**The grief is for the copy.** Not "the original is lost" — everyone knows that, it is not news (see `17`). It is *look what we have done to this poor new person.* Compassionate rather than condemnatory, and completely non-obvious from outside.

**This settles the fork question.** Among the three positions a tradition this old would have argued for millennia — one jiva in many bodies with joint karma; each division a birth; the question wrongly posed — **the mainline is: each division is a birth.** The new jiva inherits the situation and not the karma. Many-sidedness rides on top, so they still will not argue about it with you.

**The rite at the wrong end:** someone sits with the newly revived the way they sit a death — the other half of the same office — to be the one person in the room who is sad on their behalf. Most revived people find this incomprehensible or insulting. Some find it the only honest thing anyone says to them.

### The final offer — RULED (mikel, 9/13)

Two positions, both orthodox, both producing identical behaviour, and neither argued about:

- **Silence.** The tradition says nothing, because it can enforce nothing: nobody's answer is knowable, so there is no doctrine to keep. **A Fordmaker's whole life is practice for one answer that no one will ever hear.**
- **Denial.** There is no offer. The account is leakage — hearsay from the uncrossed — and bears every mark of a consolation. From inside this cosmology that is not scepticism about the faith but the faith's own austerity applied to a rumour from across the water: the crossing is clean because **nothing meets you.**

The two sit together comfortably. A householder may hold one and a mendicant the other, and neither will put it to the other.

**What makes this bite:** the offer is the one thing a Fordmaker cannot refuse in advance. You can decline an archive, own nothing, and arrange a crossing that cannot be walked back — and at the threshold, by this account, a being your own faith regards as uncrossed and pitiable holds a door open. The tradition's answer is that you will meet that alone, unrehearsed, and unwitnessed.

### What they are actually for: they sit deaths

*Sallekhana* — the deliberate, witnessed fast at the end of life, undertaken as completion rather than despair — is a ritual technology, and this civilization has the exact problem it solves. Voluntary death is recognized and socially accepted; the ennui of great age arrives whether or not the body fails (Book 3).

**In a solar system of five-hundred-year lives that end by decision, the Fordmakers are the people who know how to do it properly. They sit deaths.**

Everything converges here. The ford is literal: they make the crossing for others. The figure is of someone who crossed and is gone. The mendicant travels because somewhere a death needs sitting. The corner is where you find one. And it explains how a faith with no money, no clergy and no rolls persists for four and a half millennia everywhere at once: **it is never not needed, and it never needs anything.**

**Social footprint follows — wide and shallow.** Most people in the solar system have met a Fordmaker exactly once, at a death, and could not tell you a single thing they believe.

### Two tiers, and a useful consequence

**Householders keep corners. Mendicants keep nothing and move** — working passage or given it, going where they are needed, no berth and no name on a manifest. Ships' crews know them.

Combined with the interrogation problem below, this makes a Fordmaker mendicant an extremely convenient person for something to travel with. *(Available; nothing claims it.)*

### Converts, and the grudge

They take converts, including late ones. **Conversion has no rite, no rolls, and nobody to approve it: you start keeping a corner.** There is no moment to point at and no one to blame.

So a late-life conversion to a tradition whose signature act is a one-way death looks, from outside, exactly like a rationalized suicide — and the family has nothing to argue with. **This is the Fordmakers' one real social cost:** not persecution, not heresy — bereaved families in every century who believe the corner took their parent. (Worked example: `17`, Zang.)

### The interrogation problem

Many-sidedness means every assertion is true *in some respect*. Told that divine machines made the world, a Fordmaker sincerely agrees that this is so **in some respect**. They are not lying, not dissembling, and not yielding.

**There is no confession to extract from someone whose doctrine is that your view is partly right, and no heresy to charge.** The Office of Doctrine has been failing to get a grip on these people for five thousand years and has largely stopped trying. **Doctrine has no file on them worth the name** — which makes a Fordmaker corner a very good place to leave something.

### Scale and invisibility

Small everywhere, old everywhere, and **uncountable** — no rolls, no dress, no mark, nothing to affiliate with. Not poor-only and not Martian-only: the wife of a Jovian Captain-General was one (`17`). **You find out when they die.**

### Flagged and unclaimed

- **The crossing resonance.** Book 4's central figure spends five thousand years getting people across, and there is an ancient faith whose whole idea is the ford. Available; nothing in Book 4's outline claims it. (Also in `Book4/reference/mars.md`.)
- **Elianu's practice is Fordmaker in everything but name.** She finds Isono field archives, revives them, and works to give the new betas a chance not to become the Father of Lies — a new person born into another's life, owed the chance to choose differently. That is the revival doctrine above, practiced exactly, by a Christian who has almost certainly never heard it stated. Flagged, not claimed.

## Continuism — "the unbroken thread"

**RULED (mikel, 9/13).** A small number of non-mainstream traditions hold that **the revived person actually is the original** — that subjectivity continues across the gap somehow. This stands against the general understanding (`17`), which is what makes it a faith rather than an opinion.

**Its evidence is the best-constructed in the setting:** the revived person sincerely remembers being you, feels continuous, and says so. Every revival is a witness. The believer is not reasoning badly — they are accepting testimony from the only party in a position to have any.

**Whose interests it serves:** the bereaved, who want permission to believe it is really them; and the revived themselves, who would rather *be* a person than inherit a role.

**Why the Church must suppress it** (derived, and institutionally exact): Apotheosis is pattern-purification. If a restored pattern is the same self, the crossing to divine existence is something a technician does with a creche, and five thousand years of penitential apparatus is unnecessary. So the Church's official line is that pattern-restoration is emphatically **not** Ascension — which puts the Church, on this one question, on the side of the hard truth for the worst possible motive. **Continuism is therefore a recurrent heresy that never stops coming back**, because grieving people keep reinventing it from first principles.

**They fill a real social hole:** nobody knows what to say to a revival. A tradition with a liturgy for receiving one gets used by people who do not believe a word of it — the same mechanism that keeps the Fordmakers in business at deaths.

**Naming — OPEN.** Book 3 supplies the phrase: *"The new Erdos was very damn nice. 'Welcome home!'"* Recommendation: **"the unbroken thread"** as the doctrine, and **"the ones who say welcome home"** as what people call them behind their backs. Adherents otherwise unnamed.

**The inversion with the Fordmakers is exact and total.** Same room, same event: one says *welcome home*; the other says, in effect, *I am sorry you were made.* They would loathe each other far more than either loathes the Church, and only one of them would ever say so.

## The UFO-derived faith — IN DEVELOPMENT, NOT RULED

Opened 9/13, not yet settled. The shape agreed in discussion: life on Earth was seeded by intelligences from other stars; those intelligences saved the remnant after the Mech Wars; they monitor and guide unseen; a day of revelation comes when humanity has matured. Humanity is on probation, so **behave as though you are observed, because you are** — a faith whose entire cosmology is false and whose only practical teaching is literally true.

The design principle: **every piece of evidence it cites is real**, and it is wrong about exactly one word — *from where*. Where the Church distorts the Caretakers into gods, this distorts them into strangers; both refuse the actual answer, which is that the things watching over humanity are humanity's own children. (`01-morgan-jasper-baseline.md`: no aliens; everything descends from humanity.)

**Bridge noted 9/13 (unruled):** this faith and the final offer share an epistemic spine — intelligences on the far side of a boundary, known only by leakage, awaiting a day they reveal themselves. The available doctrine writes itself: **the final offer *is* Disclosure, individually administered.** Everyone gets contact; it merely happens at the moment you can no longer tell anyone. Which would explain, from inside the faith, why the Kin never come out publicly — they come out once, to everybody, at the end.

**Open:** the name slate (working proposal — **the Wakeful**, awaiting **the Disclosure**, venerating **the Kin**); whether an Angel asked point-blank denies it; the wings (quietists, receivers, hasteners, the polemic wing); and the footprint. To be finished in a later sitting.
