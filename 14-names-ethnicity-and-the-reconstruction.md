# Names, Ethnicity, and the Reconstruction

**Source**: mikel, 2026-09-12 (Kestrel Book 4 sitting; ruled while settling the book's remaining character names). Ruled canon; derived items marked.
**Scope**: system-wide — the entire civilization, not one world.

---

## The rule

**The Mech Wars disintegrated ethnic and linguistic identity, and the Caretakers' reconstruction remixed what was left past recognition.** There is no surviving population whose names, language, or descent map onto a real-world tradition. Names in this civilization are drawn from a thoroughly blended pool.

**Therefore, when naming anyone: a name should not read as belonging to any one real-world people, language, or region.** The house register is blended — consonant-rich, two or three syllables, vowel patterns that do not resolve to a single source. The existing cast is the calibration: *Khofir, Timoron, Leukas, Dravec, Verhaeren, Wracket, Elianu, Esgar, Jaemon, Burrel, Okafor, Yaug, Zang, Angier, Yarrow, Keryx, Maltis, Auron, Serik* — and the place names *Bahroz, Woaradan, Waldorf, Downcanyon*.

## What the rule does not mean

- **Not that every name must be invented.** The pool is remixed, not erased, so a name may land close to something a present-day reader recognizes purely by coincidence. What must not happen is a *cluster* — a character whose given name, surname, and manner all point at one real tradition, which would imply a continuity the Mech Wars destroyed.
- **Not that surnames and given names must match.** They routinely will not, and the mismatch is the reconstruction showing. A blended given name against an old-sounding surname is ordinary, not an error.
- **Not retroactive to the ancient dead.** People who predate the Mech Wars carry the names of their own era, and those *should* read as recognizably of one tradition, because their world still had traditions. **Kestrel Book 4's archived occupant, Tomas Adeyemi (c. four and a half millennia old), is the worked example: his name is correct precisely because it is not in the house register, and it must not be "corrected" into it.** The same applies to any recovered archive, any revived ancient, and any institution or text surviving from before the Wars.

## Consequences for writing (derived)

- A crowd's names carry no information about where anyone is from. Regional or planetary identity has to be shown some other way — physique, accent, how a body moves in the local gravity, what someone is *used to* — never by a name.
- Conversely, a name that *does* read as clearly of one tradition is a signal, and readers will take it as one: great age, an affectation, a revival, an institution that has kept an old form on purpose. Spend it deliberately.
- **Soft ethnicity remains real and visible.** The reconstruction blended descent; it did not make everyone alike. Populations differ by world and by gravity well — Kestrel Book 4's Martians are thick-bodied, short and wide; Earthers bigger and paler — and those differences are ordinary, remarked on, and occasionally the basis of ambient prejudice. The rule governs *names and linguistic descent*, not bodies.
