# Manipulators and Handles

**Source**: mikel, 2026-10-05 (Kestrel Book 4 sitting; ruled while working out what Elianu can do in the defense of her Village). Ruled canon; derived items marked. Physics checked in the sitting.
**Scope**: system-wide. The mechanism behind Fabric manipulator fields, the safety floor, and maglev, wherever the Fabric reaches.

---

## The organizing assumption

The setting supposes humanity becomes very good at a short list of technologies already being worked on — nanotechnology, robotics and cybernetics, computation and AI, genetics and bioengineering — and sets the stories about five thousand years out so all of them have had time to go far. Fabrication is cheap and fast, **but never cost-free or instant:** it costs something, and takes time governed by the size and complexity of the thing and the rate at which fabs can be fed. (mikel, 2026-10-05.)

## Why ambient nanosomes cannot be the muscle

At canon densities (about 10⁷ per cm³ in a body), even fully magnetized 100-nm nanosomes give a material a magnetization of about 17 A/m — a field near Earth's own, and attraction between two such objects of about 10⁻⁴ Pa. Moving a half-kilogram object at 0.2 g (about 1 N) needs roughly ten million times more. **The nanosomes are the control layer, not the source of force.**

## The mechanism — CANON (mikel, 2026-10-05)

1. **Built field sources.** Strong, controllable fields come from engineered emitters: ship bulkheads and hulls, road paving, buildings, and on Mars the surface grid built over millennia (which also generates the planet's magnetic shield against the solar wind; near the surface its field is only about Earth's, so the paving is the local emitter that lifts maglev vehicles). **Gradient forces fall off steeply with distance from their source:** manipulation is strong near engineered surfaces and weak in open air far from them.
2. **Handles in everything.** **Assimilation into the Fabric builds a manipulator system into every person's body,** alongside the circulatory and nervous systems, with nanosomes as its control system. **All accessible matter contains similar systems** — manufactured things by design, natural rock and dust seeded by assemblers over millennia.
3. **Programmable matter for shape.** Changes of shape (a graphene meteor shield reshaped, for instance) come from actuator nanosomes inside the material, working like muscle, assisted by nearby emitters — limited in speed and force.
4. **Ambient nanosomes as the control layer:** sensing, positioning, directing emitters, switching handles.

## Roads — CANON (mikel, 2026-10-05)

Paved roads carry **a higher density of transport-specific systems** — built emitters and handles in the paving — which is why maglev is faster and more efficient on paving. Off the road, vehicles ride the deeper, sparser surface grid, whose gradients are weaker at riding height. *(Derived: off-road, maglev vehicles ride lower and slower; how high a vehicle can lift is set by how fast the field gradient falls off above whatever surface it rides.)*

## Derived constraints (accepted with the mechanism)

- **Handles are active, switchable current networks** (superconducting nanowire is plausible), **not permanent magnets** — permanently magnetic bodies would be pulled at by every field they passed. A body's handles are off until its nanosomes switch them on. **Who can switch on a body's handles is a matter of Fabric privilege.**
- **Force must be spread through the body:** the handle network is anchored to the skeleton and connective tissue, like a built-in harness. Rough handling can still tear tissue.
- **Wherever the Fabric is absent (a hole), nothing can switch handles on,** so nothing can be held — the same fact as the safety floor's death inside a hole (`21-emp-weapons-and-the-fabric.md`). *(First stated for suppression bubbles, retired 10/9.)*
- **Fields cannot practically stop bullets:** stopping a 10 g steel slug at ~900 m/s within a meter needs a gradient of ~2,000 T/m; non-ferrous slugs are worse.

## Worked figures

- **Lifting a person on Mars:** about 260 N (70 kg × 3.7 m/s²) — with iron-like handle material, about 120 g at a 10 T/m gradient or 1.2 kg at 1 T/m; with current-loop handles, a couple of thousand ampere-turns, far less mass.
- **A coffee bulb aboard ship at 0.2 g:** about 1 N — a gram of handle material near bulkhead emitters at a few T/m. Easy.
