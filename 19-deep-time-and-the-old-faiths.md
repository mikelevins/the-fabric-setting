# Five Thousand Years: Deep Time and the Faiths

**Source**: mikel, 2026-09-13 (Kestrel Book 4 sitting) — *"any religions that have survived through the Mech Wars have existed for five thousand years; compare to the longest-existing real religions and the evolution each has gone through."* Derived throughout except where marked RULED; proposals, offered for ruling.
**Scope**: system-wide. Companion to `16-religions-after-the-wars.md` (what each faith *is*); this document is what happened to them.

---

## What five thousand years actually does

No real tradition has run five thousand years, but several have run two to three, and they all did the same small number of things. These are the mechanisms, not a list of analogies to copy:

1. **The central institution is destroyed, and the religion reorganizes around something portable.** Judaism lost the Temple twice; the second time the sacrificial cult — the entire point of the religion — was replaced by text and law, and the tradition that exists now is the *successor* to the one that fell. What survives is never what you would have guessed was essential.
2. **Extinction in the homeland, survival by export.** Buddhism was effectively gone from India for centuries while thriving everywhere it had been carried.
3. **The first great schism is about authority, not doctrine.** Sunni and Shia divided over succession within thirty years. Theology follows the fracture; it rarely causes it.
4. **Core doctrine is formalized centuries late, by committee, under political pressure — and is then believed to be original.** The Christian creeds are fourth-century.
5. **Purist revival is perennial.** Every few centuries someone attacks accumulated practice and claims to restore the original — and the "original" they restore is a reconstruction made from the documents they happen to have.
6. **Mystical and esoteric eruptions**, sometimes absorbed, sometimes crushed.
7. **Messianic catastrophe.** A mass movement forms around an imminent event; the event does not come. Most such movements die. The ones that survive do so by *explaining the failure*, and the explanation becomes the tradition's mature doctrine. The 1844 disappointment produced two durable institutions; Sabbatai Zevi's apostasy did not end his movement.
8. **Syncretism at the edges.** The tradition becomes something else where it meets something else, and the edge version eventually claims to be the centre.
9. **Imperial scale is not permanent.** Zoroastrianism went from the religion of successive empires to a few tens of thousands of people — while its ideas colonized everything around it. A tradition can be enormously influential in its afterlife and nearly extinct as a body.
10. **Whole branches vanish, leaving only documents.** North African Christianity is simply gone. So are the Sadducees. The Church of the East reached China and then almost nothing.
11. **Institutions invented a millennium in are believed to be ancient.** Tulku recognition is roughly thirteenth-century and is now inseparable from the tradition.

**The writing consequence:** any faith in this setting that looks the way it looked at its founding is wrong, and any faith whose present shape cannot be explained by a disaster, a schism, or a failure is underdeveloped.

## What this setting changes

Five modifiers, and each one bends the mechanisms above in a specific direction.

- **Living witnesses.** In the real world doctrine drifts because everyone who remembers dies. Here some do not. A four-hundred-year-old elder saying *that is not what we did* is an authority no real tradition ever had to accommodate. **This produces a distinctive conflict — gerontocracy against drift — and a distinctive crisis: what do you do when the witness is wrong, or dies, or turns out to be a reconstruction?** Elianu is the limit case and every tradition's nightmare.
- **Light-lag.** Fabric channels run at light speed; Mars to Luna is six to forty-five minutes each way, the outer system hours. **No faith can have real-time central authority across the solar system.** Every distributed institution must devolve decision-making or accept that the centre is permanently behind — and devolution produces autonomy, autonomy produces divergence, divergence produces schism. The Church already lives this: a prelate on a far world acts on his own judgment and is ratified afterward (Book 4 `reference/mars.md`).
- **The Fabric preserves everything, which is worse than preserving nothing** — see the section below. It is the largest single modifier and it does not work the way "perfect preservation" sounds.
- **Archives and revival.** You can wake the dead. **A recovered ancient is a bomb in any tradition**: they will not recognize what was built on them, and they cannot be argued with on the usual grounds. Whether they endorse or repudiate, the institution is damaged either way. (Book 4 is running this experiment live: the archived occupant is an early Praxis author.)
- **The final offer.** Every tradition must hold a position on it, and no position can be tested (`16` §The silence has a mechanism). **A permanent schism generator with no possible resolution.**

### The corpus problem — RULED (mikel, 9/13)

**The Fabric does not preserve *the* text. It preserves every version of everything**, across a civilization operating at solar-system scale. The consequences run opposite to what perfect preservation sounds like.

**1. Priority is undecidable, often in principle.** Not because a witness was lost, but because there is no global ordering of events across light-separated frames and a distributed record. **So "the earliest manuscript" — the foundational instrument of every real textual tradition — has no meaning here.** Stemmatics is impossible: you have all of the leaves and no trunk. Two versions sit in the record with no fact of the matter about which came first.

**2. Every canon is a *delectus* — a selection from a corpus nobody has read.** A tradition's scripture is not "the preserved text" but "the small set somebody chose," and everything else is still there: the drafts, the losing sides, the heresies, the private letters, the satires, the forgeries, the fiction. Unread, available, and capable of surfacing at any time. **Take the interpretive problem endemic to real scriptural scholarship and scale it by orders of magnitude.**

**3. Forgery is unfalsifiable and authenticity is the wrong question.** Everything in the record is genuine in the only sense the record can certify — it is really there. A forgery made three thousand years ago is an authentic three-thousand-year-old object, indistinguishable from what it imitates except by internal evidence, and internal evidence is exactly what an able forger controls.

**4. So discovery replaces corruption as the destabilizing force.** Real traditions are destabilized by loss and by variant readings. **These are destabilized by finds.** Someone goes into the corpus and comes back with something real, ancient, and inconvenient. That is a permanent structural source of schism, and it means **a tradition's stability depends on nobody looking too hard** — which in turn makes archivists, searchers and historians a class with disruptive power out of all proportion to their numbers.

**5. It rebuilds the purist revival rather than disarming it** (mechanism 5 above). A purist here is never short of material: **the corpus will always yield a genuine ancient text supporting any position whatever.** The perennial cry is not *back to the text* but **back to a different text** — and it can always succeed. Endlessly renewable, and never resolvable.

**6. It hands institutions an underrated power: deciding what counts.** Whoever determines the delectus holds the tradition. **This is an unexplored function of the Office of Doctrine** and a more interesting one than surveillance: under the corrected Makerist doctrine — virtue is whatever the Church says it is at a given moment — the Church needs, every century, ancient authority for its current position, and the corpus guarantees it can always find some. **The Church can always produce a precedent.** That is how real institutions use their archives.

### The extinction filter this creates

**In the real world a sacred text is scarce, and the scarcity is part of its authority.** Here text is infinite and free, so **authority cannot rest on possessing the true text — it has to come from somewhere else**: reception, performance, institution, or practice.

**Traditions whose authority rested on holding the right document did not survive the Fabric.** They were not persecuted; they drowned. Every surviving faith in this setting has a non-textual ground for its authority, and that is not a coincidence — it is the filter that decided who is still here.

## The Fordmakers

**They have probably died out and been restarted more than once, and it does not trouble them.** A tradition with no rolls, no clergy and no property can go locally extinct and be resumed from documents and one traveling mendicant. Their claim of continuity since before the Wars is unprovable — and uniquely, **their own doctrine makes the claim unnecessary**: the teaching was already lost, and what remains is a remembered remainder. A proven discontinuity would devastate any other tradition and would make a Fordmaker shrug.

**Their great and recurring crisis is that the world keeps trying to make them a profession.** If sitting deaths is what they are for, and everyone eventually needs it, then someone will offer to pay. Accepting violates non-possession; refusing means the service is rationed by luck and proximity. Five thousand years of that pressure produce the tradition's real history:

- **At least one Fordmaker establishment existed, and it is the tradition's wound.** An order that took endowments, acquired houses, kept registers of the dying, and ran the office properly and well — and became wealthy, hierarchical, and by its own standards damned. It was repudiated by a purist revival (mechanism 5), and the memory of it is why *nobody is in charge* is held with a ferocity that otherwise looks disproportionate. Whether it was destroyed, absorbed by the Church, or simply abandoned is open.
- **A standing minor schism between householders and mendicants** over whether keeping a corner at all is already a possession.
- **One extinct heresy worth having:** the literalist wing that identified the Fabric's total record with karmic accretion. Its texts occasionally resurface and are quietly disowned.

**Immune to the corpus problem, again, and for the same reason: they have no canon to defend.** A record containing ten thousand candidate texts is simply a larger remainder, and their doctrine already says the teaching is a remainder. The one tradition for which a find can never be inconvenient.

*(Unchanged by the extinction question — see `16`. Whichever way it is ruled, a tradition whose doctrine is that the teaching is a remainder loses nothing.)*

## Continuism

**It has no history. It has episodes.** Being the naive default with a theology bolted on (`16`), it is regenerated wherever grief is, so it cannot go extinct and cannot accumulate. Named movements rise, attract a following, get condemned or absorbed, dissolve, and reappear a century later under a different name with no knowledge of their predecessors.

Its authority is neither textual nor institutional — it is **the folk feeling itself**, which is why the corpus cannot touch it and why nothing can kill it.

**Which is the joke, and it should never be pointed at on the page: the faith that teaches the thread is unbroken is the only one in the solar system with no unbroken thread.**

Once or twice in five thousand years an episode grew large enough to be a real church for a century or two, with buildings and a hierarchy. The Church broke or absorbed each of them. Their documents survive and are periodically rediscovered by people who think they are inventing something.

## The Wakeful

**Four and a half thousand years of an eschaton that has not arrived is the single largest fact about them**, and it has done to them exactly what deferred eschatons do (mechanism 7).

- **The mainline is heavily spiritualized, and that is a scar rather than a temperament.** *Disclosure is a condition we grow into, not a date* is the doctrine of a movement that has been burned. The quietists' caution, the vigil, the burial funds, the hostels — all of it is post-traumatic infrastructure.
- **There was a Great Disclosure, and it failed.** Somewhere in the last few thousand years a date was set, a mass movement gathered across worlds, and nothing happened. **Everything careful about the modern Wakeful is a response to it**, and the movement that exists now is the remnant that explained the failure rather than the body that formed before it. The date, the world, and the leader are open; the event is not optional.
- **They are a corpus-mining tradition and always were.** Their scripture is an unbounded and contradictory body of Angel-encounter accounts — real, fabricated, and unorderable — and the receivers' wing is, functionally, a school of interpretation working the record. The devotional **search** for the prebellum person you are a beta of is the same activity in a different key.
- **The hasteners are the ones who did not learn**, and they are periodically catastrophic. At least one hastener project built something — a beacon, a signal, an attempt to force contact — and got either nothing or an answer. If an answer, it was an Angel exercising discretion (`18`), and the encounter is contested scripture to this day.
- **The extinction worth having: the branch that got it right, and therefore stopped.** A Wakeful school somewhere concluded that the Kin are the Caretakers, that the Caretakers are humanity's own descendants, and that the account was true in every particular but the origin. **Being correct converted their faith into ordinary knowledge, and the community dispersed** — traditions do not survive being right. A small remnant insisted it was still a religion and dwindled. Nobody remembers them, because there is nothing to remember: they were absorbed into the general population as people who happened to know something true that nobody believed.

## Christianity-descended

**The revivals each held their own councils, and anathematized each other** (mechanism 4). But the real-world version of this fight is about who *inherited*; here nobody inherited, so **the disputes are unresolvable in principle** and everyone knows it. That knowledge is itself a doctrinal problem, and the tradition's honest wing has been chewing on it for millennia.

- **The Teilhardian family's dominance is a transformation, not a starting condition.** A marginal speculation becomes the central form because the world came to look like it — the same way apocalyptic expectation moved from the margin to the centre of a first-century Jewish sect. Worth remembering that it was once a footnote.
- **The revivals diverged by *choosing* differently, not by losing anything** — each made its own delectus from the same infinite shelf, which is the mechanism behind their mutual unrecognizability. And any of them can be destabilized at any time by someone finding something. **The worst-hit tradition in the setting.**
- **A whole regional branch is simply gone.** Pick a world and let it be Venus or the Belt: a Christianity that flourished for centuries and is now known only from its own documents, like Augustine's church. Nobody mourns it because nobody remembers it existed.
- **The anti-idolatry wing has been martyred repeatedly and keeps coming back**, because its claim is true and independently rediscoverable. The truth regenerates the heresy — the Church cannot kill an argument that any thoughtful person can reconstruct.
- **Elianu is the tradition's nightmare and its unclaimed prize.** A living witness who predates every council, every creed, and every revival, who can say *that is not what we believed* to any of them, and who declines to. Any revivalist who learned what she is would face a choice between their reconstruction and a witness. *(Flagged, not claimed.)*

## Judaism-descended

**The tradition's own historical self-understanding places the Mech Wars in a series. It is the third destruction**, and it has liturgy for the shape of it — which is why it came through with its vocabulary intact while others came through with nothing.

- **The re-grounding of the covenant from descent to undertaking was itself a schism**, and the losing party did not vanish. **A descent party survives as a remnant** — a small population holding an unverifiable claim of lineage that nobody else recognizes, structurally like the Samaritans: ancient, tiny, entirely sincere, and regarded by the majority with a mixture of respect and embarrassment. Whether they are right is unknowable, which is the point.
- **It meets the corpus with the best methodology and the clearest sight of its limits.** A tradition built on scribal precision, chains of transmission, and dissents preserved beside decisions arrives equipped — and equipped enough to see that no methodology can settle priority here. **Its answer is reception, not priority:** a text is authoritative because the community receives it as such, never because it is demonstrably oldest. That is the only move available, and it already had it.
- **The majority's argument is permanent** and the tradition regards the permanence as correct rather than as a failure.
- **The setting's hardest question belongs to them and remains open:** if you are a reconstruction, are you the one who accepted?

## Islam-descended

**The first great schism is about authority, exactly as the mechanism predicts — and light-lag supplies the occasion.** Two functions require someone to be in charge: maintaining the computed direction of prayer, and ruling for people in impossible places. So the fracture is between

- a **centralist** party — there is one authority, and you wait for the answer, however long the answer takes; and
- a **devolved** party — the qualified person present rules now and is ratified or not afterward.

**This is the same fracture the physics forces on the Church** (a prelate on a far world acting without cover), arriving independently in a second tradition from the same cause. That convergence is worth keeping: institutions that share a universe share its problems.

- **It holds the one thing the corpus cannot swamp: a text that is recited, not stored.** Authority resting on a living chain of performance is immune to superabundance, because you cannot find a variant *performance* in an archive. **The break in that chain at the Wars is the tradition's wound**, and the reconstituted chain is guarded with a ferocity that outsiders read as excessive and insiders know is the whole of it.
- **A purist revival rejected all post-Wars jurisprudence as innovation** and tried to practise only what the recitation itself contains — and could not answer the qibla question, because the recitation does not address a solar system. It either collapsed or hardened into the most rigid surviving branch. Both endings are available and the second is more interesting.
- **Least-distorted does not mean unchanged.** Five thousand years of rulings for people in impossible places is an enormous accumulated body, and its very size is what the purists periodically attack.

## Buddhism-descended

- **Extinct on Earth.** The homeland pattern, transposed directly and cheaply: gone from the world it began on, thriving in the Belt and the outer system. (Earth is underdeveloped in the setting and this costs nothing to assert.)
- **The austere branch's danger is not extinction but dissolution into the ambient — death by success.** Diffused far enough that everyone sits, everyone half-knows the analysis, and it stops being a tradition and becomes a technique. **What remains as a tradition is a small body of people who insist it is more than a technique**, and who are regarded by everyone else as precious about it. This is the most likely present state and it is a good one: enormous influence, negligible institution.
- **The austere branch is the least troubled tradition in the setting**: a practice needs no critical edition. *Sit down* has no variants. Another reason it diffuses into technique rather than defending a canon.
- **The syncretic branch is delighted by the corpus.** An absorbing tradition with infinite material and a doctrine of skillful means has no reason for anxiety about provenance — it has the largest canon in the solar system and the least worry about it.
- **The syncretic branch has settled and re-opened the tulku question several times.** An era of technological verification — archive matching, pattern comparison — produced "certain" lineages and enormous institutional confidence, and was later repudiated by a movement arguing that verification proves the wrong thing entirely: that a matched pattern is precisely not what recognition was ever about. The repudiation is the more sophisticated position and it lost, or won, in different places. **Live and unresolved is the best state for it.**
- Its absorption of the **Angels** as practice-figures is itself the mechanism-8 edge-becomes-centre move, and the Church has never known what to do about it: it is not a denial of anything, it is a use.

## The Praxis

**RULED (mikel, 9/13): the Praxis has a unique and uniquely simple axis of orthodoxy — Pareto improvement plus respect for a subject's autonomy. It may be elaborated in any number of directions, but the kernel is so simple that it is hard to lose and hard to corrupt.**

That single property makes it behave unlike every other tradition in this document.

**The kernel is not a creed but a *procedure* — it is executable** (`18-the-caretakers-virtue.md` §The kernel is executable), which is the appropriate form for an ethic professed by intelligent computing machines, and which is the root of everything below.

**The kernel is short, and it is *operational*.** Two clauses, holdable in one head, and they decide cases: did this make anyone worse off by their own standards, and did we ask them? Most traditions have a memorable summary; few have one that settles an actual question. This one does the work.

**So elaboration obscures rather than corrupts.** Five thousand years of commentary accumulate with no generation dying off to prune them, and the result is not drift — the core stays exactly where it was, unambiguous, and *findable*. **The accretion is a standard library, not a theology** — worked cases, precedents, implementations, edge conditions. What the apparatus costs is accessibility: a newcomer can meet ten thousand years of gloss and never reach the two clauses. Periodic clearing is therefore a real and recurring project, and every clearing is itself a contested selection from an unorderable corpus.

**It is immune to the corpus problem — but by a different mechanism than the Fordmakers.** They are immune because they have no canon to defend. **The Praxis is immune because it has a test.** Any found text, of any age and any provenance, can be checked against the kernel without establishing priority; one that contradicts the kernel is simply wrong, and its antiquity buys it nothing. For every other tradition age is authority, so a find is dangerous. Here age is irrelevant, so **a find is just another document.**

**It reforms where others schism, and that is why there is one Praxis and many of everything else.** A tradition whose authority is textual or institutional cannot settle an internal dispute, so the parties separate. A tradition with a short checkable kernel settles it: the reformer points at the two clauses and wins, and the institution corrects. **Praxis reform movements are cheap, frequent, and usually successful** — an organization that has stopped applying the test stands indicted by its own founding statement, which anybody may pick up and read.

**Its only genuine fault line is the empirical clause**, and it is a fault line about the world rather than about the doctrine: *which entities can participate.* Two practitioners in complete agreement on the kernel can disagree sharply and in good faith about whether some being counts — and that disagreement is a question of fact, not of values, so it cannot be dissolved by appeal to the kernel. It is the same open question the Caretakers have been investigating for five thousand years (`18-the-caretakers-virtue.md`), and it is where a Praxis schism would come from if one ever did.

**The one doctrinal danger, and it is acknowledged in the tradition's own literature:** the annihilation objection. Universal Pareto efficiency is trivially satisfied if no sentient beings exist. The standing answer is that the goal is a compass and not a rule — but five thousand years is long enough for someone to have pressed the literal reading and built a wing on it. A Praxis-derived cessation heresy is available, chilling, and adjacent to Abjurer conclusions without being Abjurer in origin. *(Proposal; nothing claims it.)*

**Correction to an earlier claim in this document:** the Praxis is *not* the tradition with the most to lose from a recovered ancient. It has the least. **Its defence was never textual, so having perfect texts and unbroken transmission costs it nothing.** A revived founder meets a tradition that is genuinely glad to see him and will nonetheless check his opinions against the standard he wrote, because **an author has no authority over the kernel either.** He is a person who wrote something down, not a source of validity. The bomb does not go off — which is more interesting than an earthquake, and entirely in this setting's register. *(The live instance is Book 4's and is not developed here.)*

## The Church of the Makers — its schisms

*(This answers an open question in `morgan-jasper/reference/09-church-of-the-makers.md`: "five thousand years of institutional history must have produced major doctrinal splits — what are they?")*

Under the corrected doctrine — virtue is what moves you toward perfection, and in fact virtue is whatever the Church says it is at a given moment — **the Church's schisms cannot be about content, because content is designed to be revisable. They are about who holds the definitional power.** The perennial fight, and the one that produced most real-world schisms: **institutional mediation against direct access.**

- **The recurring heresy: that refinement can be discerned without the Church.** If aesthetic and moral perfection are real properties, a person might perceive them directly — in an Angel, in a work, in a life — and need no interpreter. This is fatal to the institution and therefore suppressed, and it regenerates in every century because the doctrine invites it.
- **A great schism over the Primate's definitional authority** — whether it is absolute, or whether it is answerable to the exemplars themselves. Light-lag guarantees the practical version of this fight never ends (a prelate rules; Tycho ratifies or does not, three quarters of an hour later).
- **The dual track on revival is a seam that has cracked at least once** (`16` §Continuism): same person for the estate, not a spiritual crossing. An institution that concedes half the folk position has conceded the harder half to argue.
- **The Office of Doctrine's own legitimacy** is a live question in any century when it is visibly captured — which, given the lineage, is many of them.
