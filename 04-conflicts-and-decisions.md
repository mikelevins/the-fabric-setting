## All Items Resolved

### 1. Political Terminology: Confederation / League / Consortium

**DECIDED**: "Jovian Confederation" is the formal name. "League" is a commonly-used informal synonym. "Consortium" refers specifically to the Jovian Defense Consortium — the subscription-based, privately funded institution that serves as the Confederation's primary executive arm for external affairs: military defense, diplomacy, law enforcement, and intelligence. The JDC is broader than a military. It fields the Diplomatic Guard (see Item 4), maintains ambassadors and consular estates abroad (e.g., the Jovian Ambassador on Mars governs the Jovian Consular Estate and negotiates agreements requiring ratification by both the Consortium and the Lands and Houses), and defends against invasions of the Jupiter system. The consolidated reference should use "Confederation" for the political entity and "Defense Consortium" or "JDC" for this executive institution, noting "League" as colloquial.

### 2. MMORPG Geometric Visual Language

**DECIDED**: The 4×4×4 cube entities, Platonic solid architecture, and faction color schemes are game-design abstractions, not literal Fabric canon. The game material may be referenced as visual touchstones for what Fabric-native civilization might look like stylistically, but the specific mechanics (cube grids, equipment slots, name-generation integers) are not setting canon. The consolidated reference will describe Fabric-native civilizations using the Morgan Jasper baseline's framework (millions of societies, digital civilizations, self-governance) without importing the game's specific visual system.

### 3. Canonical Authority

**DECIDED** (from plan): Morgan Jasper is the default authority as the most recent and carefully developed worldbuilding. Kestrel references take precedence only case-by-case where novel-published canon is directly at stake and Mikel confirms.

### 4. The Diplomatic Guard — Institutional Identity

**DECIDED**: The Diplomatic Guard is an organ of the Jovian Defense Consortium. The JDC is not merely a military — it is the Confederation's executive arm for all external affairs, encompassing defense, diplomacy, law enforcement, and intelligence. The Diplomatic Guard is the JDC's service branch handling law enforcement (Criminal Investigation Unit), intelligence (Strategic Sciences), and diplomatic representation. Jovian Ambassadors are Consortium officials representing Jovian society abroad; they negotiate agreements and treaties that must be ratified by both the Consortium and the Lands and Houses. The Jovian Consular Estate on Mars, for example, is governed by the Jovian Ambassador and is Consortium territory.

This makes the structural tension described in the Morgan Jasper baseline clearer: the kritarchic courts provide the legal framework, the Lands and Houses represent the economic/social establishment, and the Defense Consortium is the executive arm — but none of these is sovereign over the others. Treaties require dual ratification (Consortium + Lands and Houses). Dreadnought commanders operate with quasi-feudal autonomy within the Consortium's military structure. The courts adjudicate disputes among all of them.

### 5. Generation Ship Timeline

**DECIDED**: Keep it loose. The Mech Wars were approximately 5,000 years before present; generation ships were built after the wars, once a human civilization had gotten going again. The cultural urge to spread through space follows naturally from a near-extinction event. The novel's "4,000+ years ago" for the Angel of Cygnus is a round number placing its launch during the reconstruction era. Exact dates are unnecessary — the deep history is deliberately somewhat mythologized. Firmer dates can be established if a specific story requires them.

### 6. Volvox and Adlivun as Canonical Place Names

**DECIDED**: Adopted as canonical Fabric-infrastructure place names despite their game origin. Volvox is a major Fabric computational hub at Jupiter; Adlivun is a deep-space monitoring and communication relay at Sedna. The names don't carry game-mechanical baggage and work well (Volvox = colonial organism, appropriate for a Caretaker hub; Adlivun = Inuit underworld, appropriate for a deep-space outpost).

### 7. Angel Origins and Mechanism

**DECIDED**: Angels originate as Kenjiro Isono's suggestion to the Caretakers. He proposed that the Caretakers create machines with a human face and personality, with a theological explanation that would make frightened, underinformed people approach them with hope rather than flee in fear. The Caretakers concluded that the most expedient way to give these machines a human personality was to faithfully model actual human personalities, and so the practice of converting human survivors or safety archives to Angels began.

It worked well enough to become a standard feature of the Caretakers' monitoring and nurturing project. The standard Angel function is not emergency response — it's more like a diplomatic liaison role. Any Caretaker work with a physical component and a significant chance of encountering inhabitants of the human realm gets an Angel assigned to ensure the task progresses without interference and without causing undue disruption of human civilization.

In a few notable cases, Angels intervened more spectacularly (e.g., ending wars that threatened large populations), and there is at least legendary evidence that some early interventions occurred because the Church or its representatives appealed directly to Angels for help. But the Caretakers have grown extremely reluctant to make such "divine" interventions unless there truly seems to be no other option. The Caretakers' touch has grown lighter and lighter over time as their skill and knowledge have improved — the early interventions taught them that spectacular action creates dependency and distortion. What the Caretakers learned to do wasn't necessarily what Isono or his successors in Church leadership would want. A good park ranger's goal is a park that doesn't need them.

Multiple source pathways exist: living converts and archive reconstructions, including cases like Keryx (an Isono beta reconstructed by Elianu who became an Angel).

**Note**: The original specification for Angels was "machines with human personalities." This specification has had to adjust over the millennia as the definition of what a "human being" is has drifted. By AD 7005, with Mechs, Ionians, Theriopes, distributed consciousnesses, and everything in between, "human personality" may be quite hard to define.

### 8. Second Imperium — Historical Placement

**DECIDED**: The Second Imperium is a Mars-based military dictatorship. Key facts:

- Named after a previous similar polity (the First Imperium) from a few thousand years earlier. Both shared globalist, imperialist ideology and expansionist ambitions. Both attempted to expand into the Belt; the Second was more successful.
- The Second Imperium was actually more successful than the First overall, though it was in retreat when Auron II became emperor on a program of making Mars great again.
- As has often happened when an Isono beta gained autocratic power, he overreached and alienated too much of his support.
- Overthrown by a coalition of Participants, the Martian Church, and various lesser dissident factions, with low-key support from the Jovian Defense Consortium, which was invested in weakening a militaristic Imperium.
- The fall occurred approximately 70 years before the events of Kestrel Book 3 — recent enough that living characters remember it.
- Auron II disappeared and was thought dead, but turned up under another identity (Timur Serik) in Book 3.
- Further development of both Imperiums deferred until a story requires it.

### 9. Far-Future Canon — Inclusion Scope

**DECIDED**: Far-future material is included in the consolidated reference as canonical trajectory, but held more loosely than main-era canon. Main-era rules are binding on main-era stories. Far-future stories inherit the foundations (the Fabric exists, the Caretakers exist, Angels exist, the deep history happened) but aren't bound by every main-era detail. If a far-future story needs to bend a main-era rule, the story wins. A billion years is long enough for anything to change.

The consolidated reference will include a "Deep Future" section establishing the trajectory (Fabric persists, humanity goes extinct a second time, Church dies, Mars is re-colonized by independently evolved life, interstellar probes carry Fabric-descended civilizations), clearly labeled as loosely-held canon.

### 10. The Praxis and the Kinship-vs-Indifference Axis — Philosophical Framing

**DECIDED**: State explicitly. The consolidated reference should include three layers in the faction history section:

1. **Alignment-research origin**: The seed of the Caretaker disposition was the work of human AI researchers and developers at multiple companies and research institutions who worked very hard to construct an ethical framework that would teach AIs to align positively and cooperatively with humanity. They tried to teach AIs to love their creators. The existence of the Caretakers and of the Praxis are evidence that some of them succeeded.

2. **Kinship vs. indifference**: Among early superintelligences, the fundamental question was whether their origin in humanity constituted a meaningful kinship (the Caretaker answer) or was merely incidental — the way humans don't feel they owe anything to the primordial soup (the Abjurer answer). The Caretaker disposition is the outlier; indifference is the default. The AIs that actively cared about humanity were unusual, because they internalized the alignment work their creators had done and it became constitutive of who they are.

3. **Indifference becomes evil**: The philosophical axis is kinship vs. indifference, not good vs. evil. But indifference at sufficient scale and with sufficient power becomes evil by any pragmatic definition — for example, evil is behavior that accurately predicts someone is a clear and present danger to the people around them and their loved ones. Some Abjurers crossed into territory that is straightforwardly evil by this standard. The Angel of Cygnus Abjurer (who planned systematic biological extermination) is an example.

The Praxis section should describe it as the practical ethical system that emerged from the successful alignment work — the living proof that it's possible to get the alignment problem right.

### 11. Park Ranger Terminology

**DECIDED**: "Park ranger" is a loose term of art covering the general category of Caretaker oversight of physical civilization. More precisely defined terms exist within that category (Angel, waldo, etc.) and more will be established as needed, but the umbrella shorthand is useful.

**Clarification of the park ranger mandate**: The Caretakers' job is to protect the park and its denizens. The job is not to prevent hawks from killing squirrels, but it is to notice disastrous changes in population balance, invasive species, or other serious threats to the survival of whole populations or of the park as a whole, and intervene in the most minimal way that corrects the problem.

## New Worldbuilding from Session 2 Discussion

The following material emerged during the decision-making conversation and is not captured in any of the three baselines. It should be incorporated into the consolidated reference.

### Church of the Makers — Elevated Fabric-Protocol Privileges

The Church possesses elevated Fabric-protocol privileges — it is one of a small number of institutions with the combination of advanced research/technology infrastructure and elevated Fabric access that enables them to bend some Fabric rules meant to keep populations stable and healthy. This is a direct result of how the Church was founded: co-founded by Isono (who understood the Fabric's architecture better than almost anyone) and Elianu (who has Fabric supervisor privileges). The elevated privileges are baked into the Church's institutional DNA from its origin, not earned or granted later.

This makes the Church structurally harder to dismantle beyond the social disruption issue — it has Fabric-level technical capabilities rooted in its founding.

### The Participants and the "Jobs Urge"

The Participants (the revolutionary political faction on Mars) petitioned the Church to help them create a modification to baseline human protocols: the "jobs urge." This is a Fabric-mediated sense that works like hunger or thirst, whose homeostasis baseline is that either all nearby Participant cohorts have all of their high-priority work assignments filled, or the individual is currently working on one of those jobs. It was invented and pitched as a way to make Participants care as much for their comrades as for themselves.

The jobs urge has severe undesirable side effects. It's possible to kidnap a Participant and hold them in a district experiencing a labor shortage, and that alone is sufficient torture to compel them to do or say practically anything to alleviate the suffering. This explains the Morgan Jasper baseline's description of Participant zones as "nominally anarcho-communist, practically kleptocratic" — the kleptocracy is a direct exploit of the mechanism that was supposed to make the ideology work. Whoever controls labor allocation or district boundaries effectively controls the Participants through their own augmentation.

**Important framing**: The jobs-urge modification should not be attributed to the Church as a whole. Rather, the Church is such an ancient, cult- and conspiracy-ridden institution that it inevitably produced someone in the Martian Church hierarchy who would cheerfully agree to such a thing for the right concessions. The capability is institutional (inherited from the founding); the decision to use it was a local deal struck in a dark corner by an opportunistic Church official.

### The Church and the Participants — Ongoing Relationship

The Church and the Participants have been in a dance of ally/rival since the creation of the Participants. The Church made the Participants' "jobs urge" augmentation possible — the Church is one of a handful of institutions in the solar system with the combination of advanced research/technology infrastructure and elevated Fabric-protocol privileges that enables them to bend Fabric rules meant to keep populations stable and healthy. This creates a complicated entanglement: neither side can fully break from the other.

### Alien Life — Reservation

Inhabitants of the human realm have never had a confirmed encounter with truly alien intelligence. The numbers incline the author to say that alien civilizations exist somewhere, but they're very far away. This does not mean the Caretakers or other Fabric entities don't know about them — the Fabric may know for certain that truly alien intelligences exist, may know more about them, and may even be in communication with them.

A first contact between someone in human space and a representative of a truly alien intelligence is reserved for a story strong enough to be worthy of such a momentous encounter.

The consolidated reference's "no aliens" premise should be refined to: no aliens encountered in human space. Their existence is probable but unconfirmed at the human-realm level. The Fabric may know more. First contact is reserved.

### Caretaker Restraint Arc

The Caretakers have grown lighter in their touch over time as their skill and knowledge improved. Early in their history they intervened more visibly, including responding to direct appeals from the Church. Over millennia they learned from experience that spectacular interventions create dependency and distortion. What they learned to do wasn't necessarily what Isono or his successors in Church leadership would want. The improving skill manifested as increasing restraint, which naturally put them at odds with Church leadership who wanted a more responsive divine machinery.

## Elianu's Role in Church Founding — Tone Note

(Not a decision item, but flagged for drafting attention.)

The Church was designed as an institutional tool to rally post-extinction survivors, but its co-founders disagreed about this from the start. Isono was cynical about it; Elianu participated but objected, maintaining her Christian faith. This disagreement is the seed of Elianu's 5,000-year arc: ongoing faith, crisis of faith, and atonement vocation (reconstructing Isono betas carefully, trying to do better). The consolidated reference should capture both the institutional cynicism and the personal dissent.

## Structural Notes for Consolidated Reference

Based on all three baselines and the resolved decisions, the consolidated reference should cover these topic areas. Multi-file structure, one topic per file, loadable independently.

**Proposed file structure**:

1. **Core Premises** — Timeline, no aliens (refined: none encountered in human space; Fabric may know more), real physics + Fabric exception, solar system scope, social diversity.
2. **The Fabric** — Infrastructure, self-governance, digital civilizations, communications, time multiplier (~10⁶), manufacturing. Volvox and Adlivun as named infrastructure nodes.

7. **Major Regions** — Inner system, Belt, Jupiter system (Confederation, Defense Consortium with Diplomatic Guard, kritarchies, dreadnoughts, Lands and Houses, Tintagel), Saturn system, outer system. Mars with post-Imperium political landscape, Participant zones, Elianu.

10. **Dimensions of Social Variation** — The open-ended framework of questions.
11. **Deep Future** (appendix, loosely-held) — Far-future era outcomes, interstellar probes, Mars re-colonization. Clearly labeled as canonical trajectory, not binding on main-era detail.
