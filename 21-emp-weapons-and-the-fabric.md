# EMP Weapons and the Fabric

**Source**: mikel, 2026-09-23 (Book 4 sitting; ruled while deriving what Leukas could do to an aircar over the Consular Estate). Ruled canon; derived items marked.
**Scope**: system-wide. Reconciles the Book 3 dock attack (`08-book3-novel-scan.md`) with the Fabric's physical basis (`01-morgan-jasper-baseline.md` §The Fabric).

---

## The weapon

An "EMP weapon" in this setting is a **nuclear-type pulse**: a huge wash of rapidly changing electric and magnetic fields, followed by high-energy **gamma rays and neutrons** at close to the speed of light. Book 3's device was "the size of a vending cart or bigger" to deliver that flux across a dock.

## What it does

- **Macro electronics:** the field wash induces currents in conductors and wrecks ships' circuits, docks, power, drive and guidance electronics. Ships are shielded against it as a matter of course ("every ship is shielded; you have to be"); a very large pulse at very close range still does extensive circuit damage.
- **Nanosomes:** the fields do little to nanometer-scale elements, but **the gamma and neutron rain destroys them** across the irradiated volume. The local Fabric is not paused; it is **holed**.
- **Recovery:** the surviving mesh at the edges of the hole **regrows** the substrate until local services can be re-established. This takes **minutes**, and the effect and refractory period **can be precisely calculated** (a growth rate, not a protocol) — which is why a well-organized raid can be timed to the window (Book 3, Zang's reconstruction). The local Fabric recovers faster than a damaged ship, whose circuit damage is real.
- **Inside the hole, until it regrows:** no channels, no overlay, no aiming assist, no effectors, no fabrication, and **no safety floor** — anything in the air falls on rock. (Derived: it looks from inside like a suppression bubble, but a bubble suppresses an intact Fabric and an EMP destroys it.)
- **Biologicals — CANON (mikel, 9/23):** anyone unshielded in the volume is killed (and, where archived, reconstructed). **Book 3, settled:** the attackers used the pulse as an attack on Fabric infrastructure, specifically surveillance, not on personnel; the crew were inside the shielded ship and were **shot** by the boarding party; anyone who happened to be outside on the dock nearby was killed by the pulse and presumably reconstructed. Shipboard systems suffered a temporary outage and recovered quickly through shielding and nanoassembler repair.

## Directed beams and other variants (mikel, 9/24; derived items marked)

- **A pulsed high-flux gamma-ray laser coupled with a neutron beam — CANON (mikel, 9/24):** holes the Fabric along its path and cuts hull. Pulsed, because pulsing gives the highest peak output (derived: pulsed ablation is also the efficient way to cut in vacuum). Unlike the nuclear-type pulse it is **directed**: it holes a column plus a scatter halo (wide in a ship's clutter, narrow in vacuum), not a sphere, and is not visible system-wide as a flash. **How the neutron component works — RULED (mikel, 9/25):** neutrons cannot be accelerated or steered magnetically, so they are **made at the muzzle**: a deuteron accelerator (deuterons are charged, so they accelerate normally) fires into a **converter target** (lithium or beryllium) at the emitter's mouth, and the nuclear reactions throw a **forward cone of fast neutrons**. The cone cannot be focused and spreads, so across open space it is useless — **it works only at point-blank range, which is why the emitter is clamped against the target's hull before it fires**; the clamp is part of the weapon, not only a grip. **The conic spread is an advantage: it makes the Fabric hole a bit larger.** The gamma-ray laser does the cutting. *(Derived, why a neutral beam: a charged-particle beam can be bent aside by a ship's magnetic defenses — manipulators, a parasol with magnetic inclusions — and a neutral one cannot.)* Anyone unshielded in its column takes a killing dose (derived from the pulse canon above: the dose that holes the Fabric is at least a killing dose). Shielding does not stop it; against a directed beam, shielding is more hull to cut (derived arithmetic, Book 4 `reference/opening-derivation.md` §The insertion assault).
- **Non-nuclear EMP (derived, 9/24):** explosive flux-compression or high-power microwave pulses carry no gamma/neutron rain, so — the fields doing "little to nanometer-scale elements" — they **do not hole the Fabric**; they hit only macro electronics, which ships shield as a matter of course. Quiet, and much less effective.
- **Seeing a beam — CANON (mikel, 9/24):** anyone *outside* a Fabric hole can have the Fabric paint a cutting beam into their vision (direct stimulation of the visual centers), and so "see" it; inside the hole they cannot.

## Consequences for use

An EMP is therefore a **radiological weapon**, deniable to nobody and a massacre under any polity's law. It belongs to an actor who has stopped caring who notices (Serik at Solomon, at the point of killing everyone in the room and their archives after). It is not a tool of an actor in a circumspect phase. *Book 4 ledger item (rule at the standoff's derivation, not before):* what stops Leukas irradiating Elianu Village once he no longer cares — candidates: he wants the archive and the protocols intact and cannot verify a field archive's radiation hardness from outside; he wants her Fabric infrastructure, not a hole in it; there is no legal costume for a radiological attack on a treaty enclave full of guests.

## Communication cannot be prevented — RULED (mikel, 9/27)

**The Fabric makes it impossible to prevent communication.** Even rendering someone unconscious does not do it: **the unconscious person's sleep persona can be contacted through the Fabric, and it transfers its memories into the waking person's.** **The only way to prevent someone communicating is to kill them — and that works only until they are reconstructed.** *(Book 4: Elianu simply appears to the imprisoned Zang, exactly as she appeared to the crew aboard* Kestrel.*)* *(Derived, for ruling: the exceptions are places where the Fabric itself is absent — a Fabric hole, or a suppression bubble; but a hole regrows in minutes and a bubble is scarce and must be continuously sustained, so neither can hold a prisoner incommunicado for long. A captor cannot silence a prisoner; he can only control what happens to her.)*

**Open (mikel, 9/27; parked):** a hole or bubble *could* cut a prisoner off, and that is a plot hole waiting to happen — **what do the Caretakers do to keep suppression technology from sabotaging their preserve?** Not needed for Book 4 yet (Leukas wants Zang's misery visible, not hidden). **Partly answered (mikel, 10/5; ruled in Kestrel Book 4, `reference/protocol-powers.md` §The Caretakers' limit on bubbles):** the Caretakers meter suppression bubbles rather than remove them (they date to the creation of the Fabric; removal would be disruptive): **a Fabric-enforced ration** of bubble-time per period across the Church; **a cost that rises with duration** (the surrounding Fabric presses in; tens of minutes at most); and **attribution** of every activation to the grant holder. A bubble kept around a prisoner for long is therefore impractical, expensive, and signed.

## The two ways of silencing the Fabric, and how it links — RULED (mikel, October 7, 2026; from Kestrel Book 4)

- **No action in the physical realm can stop data moving along Fabric channels unless it destroys the Fabric's physical substrate,** in a volume around the target large enough that the Fabric cannot route around the damage. Even then (an EMP, for example) it works only if strong enough to damage or destroy the molecular machines of the substrate, and **the effect is temporary,** because the surrounding substrate immediately begins regrowing.
- **No computational process in the Fabric can do that.** What the Fabric does provide are **routing protocols that can restrict communications over a volume for a limited time, by supervisor-mode instructions.** That is what the suppression bubbles are: **legacy supervisor-mode instructions granted to the Church in the remote past and never rescinded,** to avoid disrupting the ecosystem. (This does not mean the Caretakers could never change their minds; they are extremely conservative about changes likely to affect the ecosystem as a whole, out of an abundance of caution.) A bubble restricts routing; it does not shut anything down.
- **The local-network model:** each body's endosystems form a local network, and the Fabric is the wide-area network joining them. A bubble takes down the Fabric's routing within its volume, but not each body's internal traffic, nor the ability of two bodies in contact to bridge their local networks.
- **How the Fabric links without contact:** besides contiguous substrate, it uses electromagnetic line-of-sight links and electric currents, whose reach and fidelity fall off rapidly with distance. *Scale (Claude's computation, accepted 10/7):* **infrared/optical micro-lasers** with apertures of roughly 100 microns (dust-mote nodes) give beams about 1% of a radian wide (about 10 cm across at 10 m); a matching receiver catches about a millionth of the power (a microwatt sent gives about a picowatt, a few million photons a second, enough for steady data), so **tens of meters, line of sight only;** **microwave masers** need resonant structures of about half a wavelength, so the smallest practical (near 300 GHz, 1 mm) is about half a millimeter (a sand grain of molecular machines), spreading about a third of a radian from a few-millimeter emitter, so **meters, but through non-metal walls;** beyond these ranges, links fall off steeply (as the square of distance or worse) unless many emitters are phased together.
- **Why a cell can't silence a prisoner:** in principle a sealed, substrate-free, conductive, opaque enclosure isolates whoever is inside (a metal box with no Fabric in it). In practice it fails: (1) **the prisoner is part of the Fabric,** an assimilated body full of endosystems and nanosomes, and since substrate regrows of itself, the prisoner seeds the walls from inside; (2) every opening leaks (doors, air, food and waste, power; microwaves through any gap larger than about half a wavelength, infrared through any crack); (3) holding it requires continuous active scrubbing against regrowth. Isolating a living person is a costly, rare, conspicuous installation, not a cell; the practical tool is the bubble, which is rationed (above).

## Why a hole heals slowly, and how an Angel can close one fast — RULED (mikel, October 8, 2026; from Kestrel Book 4)

**Two limits on natural repair.** (1) **Growth from the edges:** a dead volume holds no working machinery; the living Fabric at the boundary builds new machines and pushes them inward, so the hole fills from outside in at the rate a growth front advances (basic function in minutes in the open). (2) **Trust from the edges:** new machinery is not accepted as Fabric until trusted neighbors authenticate it, all the more where an attack has just poisoned the volume, so trust spreads inward link by link; that caution is the immune system's purpose, and why full fidelity takes tens of minutes to hours.

**What an Angel brings to the center.** (1) **Machinery:** an Angel's built body is a reservoir of working nanoassemblers recruited from the surroundings; once the attack has spent itself, it can seed growth from the middle, a second front from inside out. (2) **Trust:** as an agent of the Caretakers, an Angel carries authority the Fabric recognizes at its root and can certify new machinery directly, so the network accepts it at once. Result: basic function back within a few meters in seconds; full fidelity still follows by the ordinary process; the extent is limited by what the Angel can seed and chooses to restore.

**No one else can.** Elianu can direct nanoassemblers and seed machinery, but holds no root authority over what the Fabric trusts, so her machinery still waits for trust from the edge. Root authority over trust is what the Isono lineage hunts. *(Resonance, unspent: Isono's construction-era code reaches below the safety layers, a crude, stolen access; an Angel's authority is that kind of trust held openly, by right, within them.)*

*Book 4 case:* after the poison pill on the controller, Keryx closes a circle of the hole around the dying man so the Caretakers can reach him with the final offer (they cannot make it where the Fabric is dead, `04` §the offer); the offer is invisible; the mending is visible, and Leukas, who knows the pills' repair profile, understands exactly what he is seeing.
